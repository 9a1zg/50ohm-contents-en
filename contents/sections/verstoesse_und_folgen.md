Where there are rules, there are usually penalties too if they are not observed. Violations of the Amateur Radio Act (Amateurfunkgesetz, AFuG) or the Amateur Radio Ordinance (Amateurfunkverordnung, AFuV) are not, however, always punished with fines straight away. Some radio amateurs are hit much harder when restrictions of operation are imposed on them or even a ban on operation is issued.

In the event of continued violations of the AFuG and the AFuV, the authorisation for the amateur service can even be revoked. This is the sharpest weapon of the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA). The person concerned can still call themselves a radio amateur, but is excluded from amateur radio operation. This also explains why there is a licence in addition to the amateur radio certificate. There is no provision for withdrawing the amateur radio certificate. Once you have passed the amateur radio examination, you are and remain a radio amateur.

Pay close attention to the following question! Several of the possible answers are acts that are administrative offences. But only one of them is an administrative offence - as the question asks - under the Telecommunications Act (Telekommunikationsgesetz, TKG).

For us radio amateurs, the most important point in the TKG is frequency assignment. The provisions on fines state that anyone who uses a frequency without a frequency assignment commits an administrative offence. A radio amateur who transmits with an amateur radio installation outside the assigned amateur bands is using that frequency without an assignment and commits an administrative offence.
