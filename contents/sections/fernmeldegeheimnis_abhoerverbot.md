Section 5 of the law with the unwieldy name Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act (Telekommunikation-Digitale-Dienste-Datenschutz-Gesetz, TDDDG), formerly the Telecommunications and Telemedia Data Protection Act (TTDSG), also addresses radio amateurs. That section deals with the prohibition of interception and the duty of confidentiality for all operators of radio equipment. Only messages addressed *to the general public, an unspecified group of people or radio amateurs* may be received. Everything else is prohibited. This includes reception itself, but also using and passing on messages that are not intended for the general public, an unspecified group of people or radio amateurs: all of this violates the TDDDG.

Should you receive such messages by accident, you must keep the content of the message, and even the fact that it was received, to yourself. For ever and ever! The only exceptions are emergencies and disasters. Formally, this is what is called a balancing of legal interests: which is more important, the breach of the law in passing on the message, or help in an emergency?

Possessing and manufacturing devices that are disguised as another object and are therefore particularly suitable for secretly eavesdropping on private (non-public) conversations ("bugs") is prohibited. One example is a ballpoint pen with a built-in microphone. This prohibition applies to everyone, and so of course to radio amateurs as well.

[question:VE204]

Eavesdropping on private (non-public) conversations is a *criminal offence*. Anyone who violates the prohibition of interception faces imprisonment for up to two years. Let us look at the penal provisions of Section 27 of the TDDDG:

" (1) Imprisonment for up to two years or a fine shall be imposed on anyone who
1. contrary to Section 5(1), intercepts a message or takes note of it in a comparable way,
2. contrary to Section 5(2) sentence 1, makes a disclosure, or
3. contrary to Section 8(1), manufactures or makes available on the market a telecommunications installation referred to there.

(2) If the offender acts negligently in the cases of subsection (1) number 3, the penalty is imprisonment for up to one year or a fine."
