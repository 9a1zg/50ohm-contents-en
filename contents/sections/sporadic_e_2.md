In Class N we have already met sporadic E: small, strongly ionised areas form at a height of about $\qtyrange{100}{110}{\kilo\meter}$, which bend radio waves back to the earth and so make long ranges possible in the summer months, although when they will occur cannot be predicted.

In Class E the topic is taken a little further: since with sporadic E conditions the refraction takes place in the E region, which lies at a lower height, the maximum skip distance, at most $\qty{2200}{\kilo\meter}$, is shorter than with propagation via the F region. In return, however, the skip zone is much smaller, so contacts over much shorter distances succeed. Sporadic E is therefore also spoken of as *short skip* conditions.
