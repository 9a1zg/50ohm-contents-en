The selectivity of a receiver is determined by the bandwidth of the filters in its IF amplifier.

[question:AF205]

Their bandwidth must always suit the type of modulation to be received. For receiving SSB voice signals, for example, a filter bandwidth of approx. $\qty{2.7}{\kilo\hertz}$ is used. CW signals and RTTY can be received with filter bandwidths of $\qty{500}{\hertz}$, so that they can be clearly distinguished from other adjacent signals. FM signals need a considerably larger bandwidth of approx. $\qty{12}{\kilo\hertz}$.

Filters, with their bandwidths and skirt steepness, can be implemented technically using different concepts. The narrowest filters with the steepest skirts are crystal filters, followed by ceramic filters. LC filters have larger bandwidths and their skirts are not as steep; RC filters are not normally used in the RF section of receivers.
