Self-oscillation of an amplifier can occur when power is fed back within stages or from the output of the amplifier to its input.
It is therefore important that the input and output circuits of amplifiers are decoupled from each other as well as possible. Individual stages of an amplifier should therefore be well screened.
The higher the gain factor of an amplifier, the more the amplifier also tends to self-oscillate through feedback of this kind!

<tip>
The effect of feedback and self-oscillation is well known from audio amplifiers that are operated with a microphone at the input at events and the like. If you bring the microphone (input) too close to one of the loudspeakers (output), feedback and self-oscillation of the amplifier occur, which show themselves as oscillation/whistling. This can be counteracted by keeping a sufficient distance (decoupling) from the loudspeakers or by reducing the microphone sensitivity (reducing the gain factor).
</tip>
