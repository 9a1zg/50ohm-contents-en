In Class E we have already met the BFO (beat frequency oscillator). In the demodulator, when signals with a suppressed carrier or CW signals are received, the received signal is mixed with the BFO, also called the carrier insertion oscillator. For SSB reception the BFO must be exactly on the frequency of the suppressed carrier. For CW reception, the BFO must be a certain distance from the CW signal. This distance determines the audio frequency at which the CW signal is heard in the receiver. For CW signals, $\qtyrange{600}{900}{\hertz}$ is usually felt to be a pleasant listening frequency. The difference between the BFO frequency and the last IF should therefore be about $\qty{800}{\hertz}$ for comfortable reception of CW signals.

A BFO should have the highest possible frequency stability, so that received signals are reproduced stably and without distortion and the receiver does not have to be retuned constantly. Crystal-controlled oscillators are therefore the best suited.
