There are also competitions in amateur radio, which are called *contests*. In short, the aim of every contest is to make as many contacts as possible in a limited time. Contests are for sporting competition, but also for improving your own amateur station and operating procedures. Every contest is governed by rules, which the organiser lays down in the *contest rules*.

<indepth>
Contests take place on various bands and in various modes. Depending on the contest rules, you can contact all participating stations or only certain ones. In some contests, certain stations, e.g. from a particular country or region, bring more points. In other contests, such as the IARU Region 1 Field Day contest, you are only scored if you operate a portable station.
</indepth>

---

Calls made as part of a contest always include the word "Contest", or "Test" in telegraphy. This shows at once that the calling station is taking part in a contest. If, for example, DD4UQ is taking part portable in the popular Field Day contest in telegraphy, the call might be "CQ FD DD4UQ/P TEST".

<indepth>
In telegraphy, *TEST* does not mean that someone is carrying out a trial, such as testing their antenna; it stands for taking part in a contest.
</indepth>

It is important to read the contest rules before taking part in a contest. They contain important details of the period and the frequency ranges to be used, and of the information that must be exchanged in every contact. One contest might, for example, require the exchange of call sign, signal report and Maidenhead locator, while another contest requires a serial number or the operator's age in addition to the call sign and report.

The contest rules also lay down the rules by which the participants are judged afterwards, to determine the winner and a ranking list. If the rules are not kept, for example if you transmit at the wrong time or in the wrong frequency range, or if not all the required information is exchanged, the contacts concerned cannot be counted, or points are deducted.

In a contest, each contact and the exchange of information should be kept as short as possible, so that as many contacts as possible can be made in the given time.

There is a special rule when you take part in a *sprint contest*. In a sprint contest you may not stay on one frequency. After you have called CQ and made a contact, you must leave the frequency to the station that answered you. You yourself must look for a new free frequency to call CQ again, or you answer other stations.
