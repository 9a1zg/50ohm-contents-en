Personal call signs are the call signs assigned most often. In Germany there are three different licence classes, each with its own ranges in the Call Sign Plan. The classes, which build on one another, are Class N (Entry Level Licence), Class E (Novice) and Class A (Advanced). Table [ref:n_persoenliche_rufzeichen_praefixe] shows which prefixes and digits are assigned to the amateur radio classes. Table [ref:n_persoenliche_rufzeichen_beispiele] gives some examples of personal call signs of the 3 classes. Most radio amateurs in Germany hold a Class A amateur radio licence, which is why this block is the largest.

After passing the exam, you can submit the application for "authorisation to participate in the amateur service" to the BNetzA. Once it has been processed, you are sent the licence document with your personal call sign. Only with this licence may you start operating. The licence applies only personally to the radio amateur named in the document. It is not transferable.

[question:VC107]

---

The BNetzA's application form lets you give a preferred call sign. Beforehand, you should check in the [BNetzA call sign list](https://50ohm.de/rzl) or with the [BNetzA online search](https://50ohm.de/rza) whether the call sign is still free! Even though the request is usually granted, there is no entitlement to the assignment of a particular call sign. It can make sense to give more than one preference, since a call sign may have been assigned in the meantime or may not be available for other reasons.

<indepth>
Preferences in the *choice of a call sign* vary widely. Some radio amateurs choose a short form of their name, such as DL1FLO (*FLO*rian), or the initials of their name, DL4HR (*H*arry *R*ode). Others pay more attention to good intelligibility in voice operation, or value a suffix that is good for Morse telegraphy. Call signs with 2 letters in the suffix have often been assigned already. The first letter of your home town is then often a good choice for the third letter of the suffix, e.g. DL1ASN (*A*lexander *S*chumacher from *N*eheim). If no preferences are given, or the preferred call signs have already been assigned, the BNetzA chooses a call sign for you.
</indepth>

[question:VD208]

The BNetzA does not usually change call signs of its own accord. However, if regulations or licence classes change, it may become necessary for the BNetzA to withdraw or change call signs. Any radio amateur can, however, make a new application for authorisation for the amateur service on their own initiative. This is a way to change your own call sign.
