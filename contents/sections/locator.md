It often happens that you want to give your own location during a contact. You can do this by naming a place. But there is also another option, used especially often for contacts on VHF, UHF and higher bands: you can use a locator system. In a locator system, the surface of the whole Earth is divided into boxes, and each box has a unique designation. In amateur radio we use the *Maidenhead locator system*, which is also called the grid locator or QTH locator.

<wordorigin>
The Maidenhead locator system is named after the *town of Maidenhead*, which lies west of London in the United Kingdom. In 1980 an IARU technical conference was held there that reformed the QRA locator system used until then.
</wordorigin>

The structure of the Maidenhead locator system is explained below. At the same time, we will try to work out the Maidenhead locator of the DARC head office, which is in Baunatal near Kassel in Hesse.

---

As a first step, the Maidenhead locator system divides the surface of the Earth into 18 by 18 areas of equal size, which are called *fields*. Each field is assigned a combination of letters from AA to RR (Figure [ref:n_locator_welt]). The first letter counts up, starting with A at the 180th meridian, from west to east as far as R. The second letter starts at A at the South Pole and ends with R at the North Pole. On the map, then, the count starts at the bottom left and runs to the top right. The DARC head office lies in field JO.

<margin>
[photo:2:n_locator_jo:Field JO of the Maidenhead locator system. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, SRTM. Map style © OpenTopoMap (CC-BY-SA)]
</margin>


---

Each of these fields is in turn divided into 10 x 10 smaller areas, which are called "squares". Each square is assigned a two-digit number. As with the fields, the numbering runs from left to right and from bottom to top (Figure [ref:n_locator_jo]). Within field JO, the DARC head office lies in square 41.

<indepth>
Strictly speaking, the squares of the Maidenhead locator are not squares at all, because the Earth's surface is curved. They are called squares nevertheless.
</indepth>

Each square is now divided in turn into subsquares, which are given letters by the same logic as the fields, but from AA to XX. The DARC head office lies in field JO, square 41, subsquare RG.

| X: Name | l: German | l: Alternative German name | c: | c: e.g. |
| Field | Feld | Größtfeld | AA-RR | JO |
| Square | Quadrat | Großfeld | 00-99 | 41 |
| Subsquare | Unter-Quadrat | Kleinfeld | AA-XX | RG |

To form a Maidenhead locator for a given location, the designations of the field, the square and the subsquare are written one after the other. The DARC head office is therefore in locator JO41RG.
