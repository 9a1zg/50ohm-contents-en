With an *SWL QSL card*, a listener asks an amateur station they have heard to confirm their reception report. Figures [ref:swl_qsl_beispiel1] and [ref:swl_qsl_beispiel2] show examples of how such cards can be designed. The details must agree with the log of the station written to.

An SWL QSL card contains at least:

* your own SWL number or DE identifier
* the call sign of the station heard and written to (TO RADIO)
* date and time in UTC
* frequency or band
* mode
* the reception report
* the call sign of the station it was in contact with, often labelled *QSO with* or *Working* (WKG)

The time must be converted correctly to UTC. As a reminder: in Germany, UTC = CET minus 1 hour in winter, and UTC = CEST minus 2 hours in summer.

For sending cards direct by post, addresses can be looked up in international call sign lists, known as callbooks, or on the internet.

[question:BG110]

DARC members can send and receive QSL cards through the QSL bureau. Outgoing cards are handed in, pre-sorted, at the local club. Incoming confirmations come back the same way. For cards from abroad, this can take several months.

As recommended by the DARC and the IARU, a QSL card measures about $\qty{9}{\centi\meter}$ by $\qty{14}{\centi\meter}$ and is made of stiff card. A clear, easily readable data side is more important than an elaborate design.
