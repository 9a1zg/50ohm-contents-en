Another kind of competition is *Amateur Radio Direction Finding (ARDF)*. These are direction-finding competitions, which in German-speaking countries are mostly called *Fuchsjagd* ("foxhunt"). The "foxes" are small hidden transmitters, which the participants have to find with direction-finding receivers and reach on foot. The winner is the first to reach the finish after finding all the foxes.

The foxes take turns to transmit, for example for one minute each, each sending a different special call sign in CW Morse telegraphy. Table [ref:ardf_morse_code] lists all six call signs that the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA) has assigned specifically to foxhunt transmitters.

<tip>
You do not need to know Morse telegraphy to copy the call signs of the foxhunt transmitters. Only characters are used that consist solely of long tones (M, O) or solely of short tones (E, I, S, H, 5). They are sent so slowly that you can count along.
</tip>
