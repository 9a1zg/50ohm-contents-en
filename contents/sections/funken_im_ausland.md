With an amateur radio licence it is possible, under certain conditions, to operate abroad as well. For this purpose many states have concluded an agreement that governs operation during a temporary stay abroad and the mutual recognition of amateur radio certificates.
The agreement was drawn up by the member states of the *European Conference of Postal and Telecommunications Administrations* (Conférence Européenne des Administrations des Postes et des Télécommunications, CEPT).

The CEPT agreement consists of the Reports and Recommendations listed in Table [ref:n_funken_im_ausland_cept_empfehlungen]. Don't worry, you do not have to learn the individual recommendations by heart. For the examination it is enough to know the most important provisions. In the German examination questions, the German translation *ECC-Empfehlung* is used, or the word is simply left out.

[question:VB104]

---

Not all CEPT member states actually implement the provisions on amateur radio. Amateur radio operation may therefore be carried out only in those states that actually apply the CEPT arrangements. You must not have a permanent residence there and may stay there only temporarily, for up to 3 months.

<tip>
In the [CEPT country list](https://50ohm.de/cll) of the DARC foreign affairs department (DARC-Auslandsreferat) you can look up for each country exactly which rules apply there and must be observed.

[Become a DARC member now!](https://50ohm.de/mw)
</tip>

[question:VB107]
[question:VB109]

You need to know that there are states that do not belong to this Conference but nevertheless apply the CEPT arrangements in part or in full, e.g. the USA and Australia.

[question:VB108]

---

When you operate abroad, this must be indicated by an additional call sign prefix. Let us assume, for example, that the radio amateurs with the call signs DO7PR and DL9MJ travel to Switzerland and want to operate there. Depending on the class, a different prefix must be used in Switzerland: *HB3* for Class E and *HB9* for Class A. In Switzerland the two radio amateurs must therefore use the call signs *HB3/DO7PR* and *HB9/DL9MJ* respectively. Other countries have similar rules. Which prefix has to be used differs from country to country.

<margin>
The foreign prefix and your own call sign are to be separated by the word "stroke"!
</margin>

[question:BD213]
[question:BD214]

At present, Class N may not be used to operate abroad, because Germany has not yet notified Class N to the CEPT as an Entry Level Licence. Licence class N is therefore valid only in Germany.

[question:VB105]

Foreign radio amateurs may operate in Germany on the basis of the CEPT arrangements. Depending on which class their licence from their home country is assigned to in Germany, they must place the prefix *DL/* or *DO/* in front of their personal call sign.

[question:VB110]
[question:BD212]

---

The CEPT recommendations apply exclusively to personal call signs. Operating a club station always requires an application for a guest licence. A guest licence is also needed to operate an amateur station if the country does not apply the CEPT recommendation.

[question:VB114]
[question:VB113]

As in Germany, the individual CEPT countries have different national rules for amateur radio. Just because, for example, the $\qty{6}{\meter}$ band is open to Class A in Germany, that does not automatically mean that you may also use the $\qty{6}{\meter}$ band abroad. You have to comply with the provisions and conditions of the host country.

[question:VB111]
[question:VB112]

<tip>
If a journey takes you through a country in which your amateur radio licence is not recognised, you should find out beforehand whether radio equipment may be carried, and obtain the appropriate permission if necessary. The [DARC foreign affairs department](https://50ohm.de/ra) can help members to clarify these questions.
</tip>

If you move your residence abroad for more than 3 months or permanently, you can apply there for an amateur radio licence by a simplified procedure on the basis of the Harmonised Amateur Radio Examination Certificate (HAREC), without having to take an examination again. The German Class A amateur radio certificate is at the same time a HAREC certificate.
