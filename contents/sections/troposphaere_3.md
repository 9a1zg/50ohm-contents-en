As we have already learnt with the exam questions for Classes N and E, temperature inversions can lead to enhanced propagation in the VHF range. At the boundaries between warm and cold layers of air, radio waves can be reflected and guided back to the earth over greater distances.

Another phenomenon in this context is what is known as ducting (from *duct*, meaning roughly a pipe, channel or guide). In this mode of propagation (see Figure [ref:a_tropo]) the radio waves get between layers of air of different temperature and are guided along this natural waveguide channel. In this way even distances of well over $\qty{1000}{\kilo\meter}$ can be covered.
