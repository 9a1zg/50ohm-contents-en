Even without thunder and lightning, unwanted voltages can occur on antennas. The cause is often static charges, which can be produced, for example, by rain or hail. This can certainly be a danger to equipment and people. In the harmless case, it only leads to crackling interference in reception.

[question:EK206]

Static charges can be effectively prevented by bleeder resistors between the conductors of the antenna feeder and the earth of the amateur station. The resistors must be high-value (e.g. $\qty{100}{\kilo\ohm}$) so as not to impair the function of the antenna.
