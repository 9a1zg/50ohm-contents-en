Not every transmission that can technically be received may also be listened to. The prohibition of interception is now laid down in Section 5 of the *Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act* (Telekommunikation-Digitale-Dienste-Datenschutz-Gesetz, TDDDG). 

Radio equipment may be used to listen to messages that are intended for

* the operator of the radio equipment,
* radio amateurs,
* the general public or
* an unspecified group of people.

An SWL may therefore listen to amateur radio communication. Deliberately eavesdropping on private (non-public) conversations, on the other hand, is not permitted and can be a criminal offence.

[question:SWL001]
[question:SWL002]

If a message not intended for the listener is received unintentionally, neither its content nor the fact that it was received may be communicated to others. Exceptions provided for by law and special authorisations remain unaffected. In emergencies and disasters, the protection of important legal interests may also take precedence.

[question:SWL003]

<attention>
A freely accessible frequency or a technically suitable receiver does not automatically mean that the content may legally be listened to or passed on.
</attention>

<tip>
In practice: stay with the amateur bands, broadcasting and transmissions expressly intended for the general public. Other people's messages that you happen to receive should be neither recorded nor passed on.
</tip>
