In Germany, the amateur service is governed above all by the *Amateur Radio Act* (Amateurfunkgesetz, AFuG) and the *Amateur Radio Ordinance* (Amateurfunkverordnung, AFuV). The tasks and powers arising from these sets of rules are performed and exercised by the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA).

[question:VC101]
[question:VC104]

The AFuG distinguishes between the radio amateur and the amateur station. Within the meaning of the Act, a radio amateur is anyone who holds an amateur radio certificate or a recognised harmonised examination certificate. An SWL without an amateur radio certificate is therefore a listening amateur, but not yet a radio amateur in the legal sense.

[question:VC105]

As a rule, amateur radio communication may be carried on only between amateur stations. An SWL takes part by receiving, but does not send any messages into this communication.

[question:VC111]

---

Amateur radio transmissions can be received without a licence. To transmit on amateur frequencies, on the other hand, you need an amateur radio certificate, a licence and a call sign assignment.

<attention>
The DE examination is an internal examination of the DARC as an association. It replaces neither the state amateur radio examination nor the authorisation to participate in the amateur service.
</attention>

Other laws apply independently of amateur radio law. Particularly important for SWLs is the prohibition of interception, together with the duty of confidentiality for operators of radio equipment.
