The quality of contacts depends on several factors: transmit power, the antenna used, distance and, not least, the current propagation conditions. So it is often interesting to find out how well your own signal is arriving at the receiving station. To assess the reception quality, you give a reception report, known as a signal report [index:Signal report]. In amateur radio, the RST system [index:RST system] is generally used for this.

A report in the RST system consists of the following three values:

* R (Readability)[index:RST system:Readability]: This number gives the *readability* of the signal and ranges from 1 to 5. Readability is taken to mean intelligibility. A higher number means better intelligibility. Readability is assessed subjectively on the basis of factors such as volume, clarity, interference and distortion. Guidance for the assessment can be found in Table [ref:n_rst_r].
* S (Signal Strength)[index:RST system:Signal strength]: This number gives the *strength of the signal* and ranges from 1 to 9. A higher number means a higher signal strength. The signal strength is normally read from the radio's received-signal-level indicator (S meter). Many S meters can also show values above 9. Such values, however, are shown differently, in decibels (dB) above S9. The S value in the report is then given like this, for example: 9+20dB. This feature also makes the S meter on a radio easy to recognise. As can be seen in Figure [ref:n_rst_s-meter], the S meter is usually labelled with values from 1 to 9 followed by dB values.
* T (Tone)[index:RST system:Tone quality]: This number is only used in Morse telegraphy and gives the *quality of the tone*. In voice communication this value is left out.
