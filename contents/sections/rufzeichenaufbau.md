Amateur radio has different kinds of call signs. We already met one kind at the beginning of the course: personal call signs. In German official language they are called "personengebundene Rufzeichen" (call signs tied to the person). In Germany, the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA) assigns one to every radio amateur together with the authorisation for the amateur service. It is unique worldwide and may only be used personally by the radio amateur to whom it was assigned.

[question:VC116]

---

A call sign consists of three parts: prefix, digit and suffix. The prefix usually consists of the first two characters, e.g. DL or 4U. Sometimes, however, it is only one character long. It is followed by a digit from 0 to 9. The call sign ends with the suffix of at least one further character. Figure [ref:n_amateurfunkstrationen_aufbau_rufzeichen] gives a graphical overview of how call signs are made up.

The prefix is assigned by country and can be used to locate the amateur station. The prefixes are laid down by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) in the Radio Regulations (RR). All ITU member states are expected to implement them in national law.

For Germany, the Radio Regulations provide the prefixes DA to DR and Y2 to Y9. In practice, however, the prefixes Y2 to Y9 are hardly used.

[question:VA406]

---

German personal amateur call signs always consist of a 2-letter prefix, one digit and a 2- or 3-letter suffix, for example DL1PZ, DO7PR or DN2JMU.
[question:VD203]

Besides personal call signs, further call signs can be assigned for remotely controlled and automatically operating amateur stations or for club stations. We will come to these later in this chapter.

[question:VD202]

The BNetzA assigns the prefixes in Germany according to the [Call Sign Plan](https://50ohm.de/rzp), so that the purpose of a call sign can be recognised from the call sign itself. The Call Sign Plan lays down exactly which prefixes, digits and suffixes are used, both for personal call signs and for call signs for remotely controlled and automatically operating amateur stations and club stations.

The Call Sign Plan is provided as an aid during the amateur radio examination. It is very helpful in answering a number of questions that follow in the next sections. We therefore recommend that you become familiar with the document so that you can use it well in the exam. After the exam, you should keep yourself informed regularly so as to stay up to date with any changes.
