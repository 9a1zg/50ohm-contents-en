In the Class N course we already met the relationship between frequency ($f$) and wavelength ($\lambda$). There, two specially tailored quantity equations from the formula sheet for the exam were given for it.

$f[[\unit{\mega\hertz}]] = \dfrac{300}{\lambda[[\unit{\meter}]]}$

$\lambda[[\unit{\meter}]] = \dfrac{300}{f[[\unit{\mega\hertz}]]}$

<indepth>
Equations that already state the unit in which the values must be given are called  *tailored quantity equations*. 
</indepth>

In reality, however, this is only one equation, rearranged in the first case for the frequency and in the second case for the wavelength. 

In technical calculations we keep having to rearrange equations so that the quantity we are looking for is on its own on one side. To do this, we apply the necessary mathematical operations (multiplying, dividing, adding, subtracting, ...) to both sides of the equation *at the same time*. With a little practice this is much easier than memorising every form of a relationship separately. In Class E, and in Class A too, it is in fact essential, because the formula sheet gives the equations only in their basic form.

---

<indepth>
In base units ($\unit{\second}$, $\unit{\meter}$), the relationship between wavelength and frequency in free space is:

$\lambda = \dfrac{c_0}{f}$

Here $c_0$ is the velocity of propagation of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum ("speed of light"), $c_o \approx \qty{300000000}{\meter\per\second}$
</indepth>

Here we look at the relationship between frequency and wavelength in the more abstract form:

$\lambda = \dfrac{c_0}{f}$

Now we want to find the frequency that corresponds to a wavelength of 2.069 m.

To do this, we first multiply both sides of the equation by the frequency.

$\lambda = \dfrac{c_0}{f} \quad\quad\quad | \cdot f$

This is shown by "$|~\cdot f$", where the vertical bar means that the following operation is carried out on both sides.

This gives a new equation:

$\lambda\cdot f = \dfrac{c_0 \cdot f}{f}$

in which the frequency cancels out on the right-hand side (because $f$ divided by $f$ is 1):

$\lambda \cdot f = c_0$

Now the frequency is already on the left-hand side of the equation, where we want it. Next we divide both sides by the wavelength:

$\lambda \cdot f = c_0 \quad\quad\quad |: \lambda$

This gives:

$\frac{\lambda\cdot f}{\lambda} = \frac{c_0}{\lambda}$

On the left-hand side the lambda cancels out again:

$f = \dfrac{c_0}{\lambda}$

This is the relationship we were looking for. We put in the numerical values:

$f = \dfrac{\qty{300000000}{\meter\per\second}}{\qty{2.069}{\meter}} = \dfrac{\num{300000000}}{\qty{2.069}{\second}}  = \qty{144997583}{\hertz} \approx \qty{145}{\mega\hertz} $

Here we have used the fact that $\frac{1}{\unit{\second}} = \qty{1}{\hertz}$.

We can now rearrange formulas using multiplication and division. In Class E we will meet further formulas that also require addition and subtraction, powers and roots. Finally, in Class A, logarithms are added too. Don't worry: at each point we will explain exactly how these formulas are rearranged, step by step.
