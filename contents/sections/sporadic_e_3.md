In Classes N and E we have already learnt that contacts via sporadic E (particularly strongly ionised areas of the E region) are made mainly from the upper HF range up to the $\qty{2}{\meter}$ band. 

[question:AH301]

The higher the frequency, the rarer sporadic E propagation is. At times of medium to high solar activity, when propagation via the F region is possible, especially on the $\qty{10}{\meter}$ band, sporadic E tends to be a disadvantage on HF, because it prevents the radio waves from getting through to the F region, so that skip distances are limited to at most $\qty{2200}{\kilo\meter}$ and the skip zone can also disappear.
