When inductors are connected in series, their inductances add up.

$L_{\mathrm{ges}} = L_1 + L_2 + L_3 + \dots$

with the total inductance $L_{\mathrm{ges}}$ and the individual inductances $L_1, L_2, L_3, \dots$

This can be pictured by simply imagining several similar inductors one after the other. The turns of the coil then add up. Electrically, the combination of two identical inductors acts like a correspondingly longer coil.

<indepth>
[photo:291:a_Spule mit Anzapfungen:Coil with 14 taps in a home-built antenna tuning unit]
The sections between the taps can be regarded as individual inductors, whose inductance can be increased in steps by switching between the taps. The series connection of all the sections then gives the inductance of the whole coil.
</indepth>

Incidentally, when inductors are connected in parallel, the reciprocals of the inductances add up:

$ \frac{1}{L_{\mathrm{ges}}} = \frac{1}{L_1} + \frac{1}{L_2} + \frac{1}{L_3} + \dots $
