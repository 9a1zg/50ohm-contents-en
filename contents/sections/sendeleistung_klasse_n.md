Since we have just been looking at radiated power, we should make a short detour to the maximum permitted radiated power for Class N radio amateurs. This is stated in ERP or EIRP and is $\qty{10}{\watt}$ ERP on the $\qty{10}{\meter}$ band and $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP on the $\qty{2}{\meter}$ and $\qty{70}{\centi\meter}$ bands.

Let us look at this with an example. Suppose that, as a Class N radio amateur, we use a VHF handheld with $\qty{5}{\watt}$ transmit power and ask ourselves whether we may connect a small Yagi-Uda antenna to it. Whether we may depends on how large the gain factor of the antenna, and therefore the equivalent isotropically radiated power, is.

Remember: the radiated power is the transmit power multiplied by the gain factor. If the gain factor relative to the isotropic radiator is less than $\num{2}$, we get a radiated power below $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP and may use the antenna. If, however, the gain factor relative to the isotropic radiator is greater than $\num{2}$, we get a radiated power above $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP and, as Class N radio amateurs, may not use the antenna.
