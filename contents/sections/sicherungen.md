As we already learnt in the chapter on the power supply unit in the Class N course, fuses are needed to protect the electronics in the event of a short circuit or overload. If we now want to replace a blown fuse, this only makes sense after the protected parts of the installation have been repaired or checked. When choosing the fuse, we make sure that it has the same tripping characteristic and the same current rating.

[question:EK204]

Our equipment is mostly protected by miniature fuses, a subgroup of fuses. But there are various kinds of fuse, such as:

* Automotive blade fuses
* TR5, a fuse for soldering onto the circuit board
* Diazed/Neozed
* High-voltage fuses

Figure [ref:e_flachstecksicherungen] shows automotive blade fuses, which are also used in amateur radio. With commercially made radios, these fuses are built directly into the cable in a fuseholder (see Figure [ref:e_sicherungshalter]).
