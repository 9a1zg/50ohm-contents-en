For many radio amateurs, repairing their own equipment is a point of honour. But in all the eagerness to get on with the job, you must not forget your own safety or make the existing damage worse. 

Every repair begins with troubleshooting. It goes without saying that the equipment is first disconnected from the mains. After that, it can be opened. But you are still not safe from an electric shock. The capacitors in the power supply remain charged even without the mains supply. 

Particularly with the switch-mode power supplies for transistor PAs that are usual today, the voltage present at some capacitor terminals is higher than the nominal mains voltage. With valve PAs, the voltage is considerably higher still! That is life-threatening! 

Without further intervention, the charge would only dissipate gradually. To be safe, we can discharge the capacitors. Simply short-circuiting them is not a good idea. Done that way, a fatal electric shock is quite possible. In the less serious case, the high short-circuit currents can destroy the capacitors and other components and start a fire. 

With a high-value, insulated resistor, the discharge takes place slowly and safely. The power rating of the resistor must be matched to the capacitance of the capacitor.
