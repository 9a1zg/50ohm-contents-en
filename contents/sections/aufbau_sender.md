Figure [ref:aufbau_sender] shows the components from which an AM transmitter can be built. We already know some of the blocks from the receiver; others are new:
1. Microphone: The microphone converts the sound waves of speech into audio-frequency electrical oscillations. Instead, e.g. for digital modes, the audio-frequency signal from the audio output of a computer can also be used.
2. Audio-frequency amplifier: The signal from the microphone or computer is first amplified.
3. Mixer: The mixer combines the radio-frequency carrier generated by the oscillator (4) with the audio-frequency oscillation from the microphone or computer. As a result, the radio-frequency carrier is amplitude-modulated with the speech or data signal.
4. Oscillator: The oscillator generates the radio-frequency oscillation at the frequency on which the transmission is to take place, for example $\qty{29.5}{\mega\hertz}$.
5. Band-pass filter: Because of the way it works, the mixer produces not only the wanted frequencies but also other, unwanted frequencies, and these have to be blocked with a band-pass filter.
6. Radio-frequency amplifier: The radio-frequency signal is now amplified so that it has the desired transmit power.
7. Low-pass filter: Since the amplification can also produce unwanted frequencies, the signal has to be filtered once more.
8. Antenna: The radio-frequency signal is now fed to the antenna, which radiates it as a radio wave.
