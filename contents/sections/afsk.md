A special form of digital modulation is audio frequency-shift keying (AFSK). Unlike in ASK, the "A" here does not stand for amplitude but for audio, that is, audible frequencies (AF): frequency-shift keying (FSK) is carried out in a range well below $\qty{20}{\kilo\hertz}$. Often the range from approx. $\qty{300}{\hertz}$ to $\qty{2700}{\hertz}$ is used.

An AFSK signal generated in this way can be transmitted just like human speech, whether by telephone or by voice radio. For transmission by radio, a further modulation has to take place, for example FM, AM or SSB. If SSB is used, the signal on the band can no longer be distinguished from an FSK signal.

<indepth>
*APRS* (Automatic Packet Reporting System) is a digital mode in amateur radio with which position, status and short messages from radio amateurs are automatically transmitted and relayed by radio and often also displayed via the internet, see [APRS.fi](https://50ohm.de/aprs). On $\qty{144.800}{\mega\hertz}$, radio amateurs use AFSK with FM radios for APRS transmissions, which are relayed by digipeaters. 
</indepth>

AFSK usually achieves only low data rates. This means that only a small amount of data can be transmitted per unit of time. In return, it works with almost any radio. A microphone and loudspeaker connection is all that is needed. For APRS on $\qty{144.800}{\mega\hertz}$, for example, a data rate of $\qty{1200}{\bit\per\second}$ is used.
