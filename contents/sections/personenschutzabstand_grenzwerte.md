%Frequenzabhängigkeit des Personenschutzabstands:

The [Federal Office for Radiation Protection (Bundesamt für Strahlenschutz)](https://50ohm.de/bfs) provides information on its website about the biological effects of RF fields on the human body.
  
* RF electromagnetic fields are absorbed by the body.
* How much energy is absorbed depends on the strength and the frequency of the electromagnetic fields.
* Force effects and a heating effect of RF fields have been clearly demonstrated.
* The heating effect is decisive for possible effects on human health.

What matters for answering the question here is point 2: the energy absorption of the human body depends on the frequency.

% Zeitabhängigkeit des Personenschutzabstand:

The [Ordinance on Electromagnetic Fields (26. BImSchV)](https://50ohm.de/BImSchV) describes the limits in Annex 1 (Tables 1a and 1b) and in Annex 3. It distinguishes between three cases:

* Instantaneous peak value (in $\unit{\kilo\volt\per\meter}$ against $\unit{\hertz}$, see Figure [ref:e_grenzwerte_max])
* 6-minute intervals (in $\unit{\volt\per\meter}$ against $\unit{\mega\hertz}$, see Figure [ref:e_grenzwerte_avg])
* Pulsed fields (calculated from the first two and frequency-dependent factors, see Figure [ref:e_grenzwerte_pulse])

Since we do not transmit all the time, we usually use the RMS value of the field strength ($\unit{\volt\per\meter}$), averaged over a period of 6 minutes. An important value here is $\qty{28}{\volt\per\meter}$, which applies to the range $\qtyrange{10}{400}{\mega\hertz}$, that is, to a large number of amateur bands, and which appears in several of the following examination questions on the protection of persons.

<tip>
The limits do not have to be learnt by heart for the examination; they are always given in the question.
</tip>

[question:EK102]

An exception here is active implants (e.g. pacemakers, insulin pumps, cochlear implants), since in certain cases the maximum instantaneous values must be assumed for them.
