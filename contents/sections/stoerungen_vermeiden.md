Despite all the measures to avoid unwanted emissions and to ensure electromagnetic compatibility, electronic equipment may still suffer interference or breakthrough. This can happen for three reasons:

* unwanted frequency components that are not sufficiently suppressed
* equipment that is inadequately screened or inadequately earthed 
* the wanted emissions themselves

<indepth>
A distinction is made between interference (in German *Störung*) and breakthrough (*störende Beeinflussung*).

Interference is present when the amateur station radiates unwanted frequency components to such an extent that the limits are exceeded and interference results. An example would be the emission of a harmonic of an HF transceiver in the FM broadcast band (VHF).

[picture:745:stoerungen_vermeiden_oberschwingung:Interference to a television set's DVB-T2 reception from the harmonic of an amateur radio transmission]

</indepth>

---

Interference to equipment, or breakthrough, can of course be a nuisance to the people affected. If the problems only occur in your own household, you may still be able to live with them. If, however, the neighbours' equipment suffers interference, things become more difficult.

If we hear of such a problem in our surroundings, we should always consider that it might be connected with our amateur radio installation. A radio amateur should therefore offer in such cases to check whether they are the cause.

[question:NJ102]

<indepth>
With breakthrough, on the other hand, there is an adverse effect, for example on broadcast reception, even though the radio amateur keeps within the limits for unwanted emissions. Breakthrough can reach the affected equipment by three different routes, which can occur either singly or together:
* radiated pick-up via a receiving antenna
* direct pick-up into electrical equipment
* conducted pick-up via connecting leads

You should now look for the cause and check whether you can remedy the problem with your own resources. It may be, however, that the cause cannot be found or the problem cannot be eliminated. Then it is advisable to point out to the neighbour that they can contact the radio interference reporting service (Funkstörungsannahme) of the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA).

<tip>
Finding the cause of electromagnetic interference can be complicated. Members of the DARC can get support from the *electromagnetic compatibility officers*, whom the DARC has in its districts and also in many of its local clubs.

[question:VE302]

Finding the cause can take some time. To keep good relations with the neighbours, it can therefore make sense to reduce your own transmit power to the point where no problems occur.

[question:VE301]

If the check by the BNetzA shows that the problems with the affected equipment are caused by the amateur radio transmissions, there are three possible cases to distinguish.

In the first case, it is found that the amateur radio installation is not being operated in compliance with the regulations, for example because the unwanted emissions are too strong. In this case the BNetzA is entitled to order a restriction of operation subject to a fee. This may be, for example, a limit on the transmit power.

The second case is as follows: the amateur station is being operated in compliance with the regulations. A measurement of the field strength produced at the location of the affected equipment shows that the field strength is lower than the field strength that the affected equipment must be able to withstand under its immunity requirements.

The interference is therefore caused by the affected equipment not meeting the prescribed immunity. In this case the responsibility for eliminating the interference lies solely with the operator of the affected equipment. The radio amateur may continue transmitting unchanged.

[question:VE305]
[question:VE306]

In the third case, the amateur station is likewise being operated in compliance with the regulations. The affected equipment, however, does meet the prescribed immunity. The interference therefore results from the field strength produced by the amateur station at the location of the affected equipment being greater than the immunity limit of the affected equipment.

---

This does not automatically mean, however, that the radio amateur has to reduce the transmit power. Rather, it is a case of conflict, since both the amateur station and the affected equipment are being operated in compliance with the regulations. In such cases the BNetzA is empowered to bring about a solution *in cooperation with all those involved*.

<attention>
Under the following circumstances, the BNetzA is empowered – even though an amateur radio installation is being operated in compliance with the regulations – to order measures without the cooperation of all those involved:
* to protect receiving and transmitting equipment that serves safety purposes
* to protect public telecommunications networks, for example the telephone network
* to protect the life and limb of a person, or property of considerable value
</attention>
