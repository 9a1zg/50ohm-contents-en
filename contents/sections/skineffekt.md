Not only a dielectric, where there is one, but also ohmic losses in the metal of the conductor contribute to the losses on feeders. These ohmic losses also increase with rising frequency, because as the frequency rises the current flows more and more only at the surface of the conductor. We call this effect the "skin effect".

<indepth>
The *cause of the skin effect* is that high-frequency electromagnetic fields cannot penetrate deep into a metallic conductor, because they induce currents in the conductor that oppose the field. In an ideal conductor the interior would always be completely free of field, and the current would flow only at the surface.
</indepth>
