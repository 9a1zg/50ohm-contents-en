The distance between two crests or two troughs is called the wavelength [index:Wavelength]. The wavelength depends on the frequency. The higher the frequency, the shorter the wavelength. The wavelength is denoted by the Greek letter $\lambda$ (lambda) and is usually given in metres ($\unit{\meter}$).

[question:NB403]
[question:NA205]

The relationship between frequency and wavelength follows from the speed of light, $\qty{300000}{\kilo\meter\per\second}$. A wave with a frequency of $\qty{1}{\hertz}$ travels $\qty{300000}{\kilo\meter}$ before one crest is followed by the next. A wave with a frequency of $\qty{1000}{\hertz}$ travels only $\qty{300}{\kilo\meter}$ before one crest is followed by the next. At $\qty{1000000}{\hertz}$, i.e. $\qty{1}{\mega\hertz}$, it is only $\qty{300}{\meter}$.

This gives the following formulas, with which you can easily convert between frequency $f$ (in $\unit{\mega\hertz}$) and wavelength $\lambda$ (in metres):

$f[[\unit{\mega\hertz}]] = \dfrac{300}{\lambda[[\unit{\meter}]]} \quad\quad\quad \lambda[[\unit{\meter}]] = \dfrac{300}{f[[\unit{\mega\hertz}]]}$

Both formulas are also on the formula sheet that is provided as an aid in the exam.

So if you divide 300 by the wavelength in metres, you get the frequency in $\unit{\mega\hertz}$. And the same the other way round: if you divide 300 by the frequency in $\unit{\mega\hertz}$, you get the wavelength in metres.

So if, for example, we want to calculate the wavelength for the frequency $\qty{145.3}{\mega\hertz}$, we put it into the second formula and solve:

$\lambda[[\unit{\meter}]] = \dfrac{300}{f[[\unit{\mega\hertz}]]} = \dfrac{300}{\qty{145.3}{\mega\hertz}} \approx \qty{2.06}{\meter}$

It works the same way the other way round. If we put the wavelength of 2.06 m into the first formula, we get the original frequency:

$f[[\unit{\mega\hertz}]] = \dfrac{300}{\lambda[[\unit{\meter}]]} = \dfrac{300}{\qty{2.06}{\meter}} \approx \qty{145.3}{\mega\hertz}$
