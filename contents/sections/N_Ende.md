Congratulations! You have now successfully completed the course. If you have not already done so, you should now prepare specifically for the amateur radio examination with a learning app or an exam trainer. The DARC offers the training app [50ohm](https://app.darc.de) for this, for example. You should also register for the examination as soon as possible, as the available dates are often limited. You will find all the important information on registering and on how the examination works under the menu item [Examination](https://50ohm.de/pruefung.html). Once you have passed the examination at the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA), you can then apply for your personal call sign and officially take part in amateur radio operation.

This course has been made available to you free of charge by the DARC e. V. and was [developed by many volunteer contributors](https://50ohm.de/infos.html#contributors). The DARC is the largest amateur radio society in Germany and represents the interests of radio amateurs to the authorities, politicians and other radio services. [Membership of the DARC](https://www.darc.de/mitgliedschaft/mitglied-werden/) means actively supporting this work: from training and encouraging young people, through technical development, to safeguarding our frequencies and modes. Anyone who wants to preserve and develop amateur radio in the long term will find in the DARC the common organisational basis for doing so. We would be delighted if you became involved in our society in future and helped us take the community forward.

The easiest way to get in touch with other radio amateurs near you is to look for a DARC local club (Ortsverband, OV). On this [page](https://50ohm.de/ov) you can find the nearest OV by your postcode. Every local club has what is known as a *district local club identifier* (Distriktsortskenner, DOK), for example *B17* or *D23*. The letter stands for the district, the number for the serial number of the local club within that district. The DARC is divided into [24 districts](https://www.darc.de/der-club/distrikte/) in all. The local members can make it easier for you to get started in the hobby and can answer many practical questions. There are also numerous ways to exchange ideas online, such as the [DARC-Chat](https://chat.darc.de), the [DARC-Treff](https://treff.darc.de) or the DARC's own [Mastodon instance](https://social.darc.de), through which you can connect with other radio amateurs.

As soon as you have passed your examination, a wide range of exciting fields opens up in which you can explore to your heart's content. From contests to home construction, emergency communications or portable and outdoor operating – to name just a few – there are numerous activities to take up as a newly licensed radio amateur. The motto is often: *after the exam is before the exam*, because there is still a great deal to discover and learn, far beyond the examination material itself. The DARC also supports this with various formats for further learning and exchange, such as the [Dienstags-Treff](https://treff.darc.de) (Tuesday meeting) or the [AFUBarcamp](https://afubarcamp.de). In addition, DARC events such as *HAM RADIO* in Friedrichshafen on Lake Constance, with more than 10,000 like-minded visitors, or the *Funk.Tag* in Kassel offer excellent opportunities to meet in person and get to know the amateur radio community. Another fine opportunity to try out as many areas of amateur radio as possible is the annual [HAM-Challenge](https://www.hamchallenge.org).
