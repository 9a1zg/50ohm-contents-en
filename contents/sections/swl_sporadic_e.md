Above $\qty{30}{\mega\hertz}$, radio waves normally travel more or less in straight lines. Long ranges are still possible when special conditions occur.

With *sporadic E*, small, strongly ionised areas form temporarily in what is known as the E region of the ionosphere. Radio waves are refracted at them, as shown in Figure [ref:swl_sporadic_e_ausbreitung]. On the $\qty{2}{\meter}$ band, this can suddenly make stations at distances of about $\qtyrange{1000}{2000}{\kilo\meter}$ audible.

Sporadic E occurs particularly in the summer months, but it cannot be reliably predicted for a particular place and time. Openings sometimes begin very suddenly and end just as quickly.
