Before we went into radiated power, we discussed that verification of compliance with the limits for the protection of persons is only required once a certain radiated power is reached. Now that we know what EIRP means, we can also put a figure on it: for fixed amateur stations the verification procedure only has to be carried out if the transmitting equipment reaches a radiated power of $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP or more.

<indepth>
*Splitting hairs*, it should be pointed out that Class N radio amateurs are entitled to operate transmitting equipment with up to $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP, and that verification of compliance with the limits for the protection of persons is already required from $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP. So if you operated transmitting equipment with exactly $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP, you would be obliged to provide the verification to the authority. In practice, however, you should not go right up to the maximum anyway, but stay at least slightly below it. That way you avoid accidentally exceeding the permitted radiated power through measurement errors or other inaccuracies. At the same time, there is then no obligation to carry out the verification procedure.
</indepth>

The notification of compliance with the limits for the protection of persons for an amateur station with more than $\qty{10}{\watt}$ EIRP, under the verification procedure for limiting electromagnetic fields (BEMFV), must be submitted to the competent regional office of the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA) before the amateur radio installation is put into operation.

<tip>
The competent regional office can be found on the [website of the Federal Network Agency](https://50ohm.de/so).
</tip>

The central element of the verification procedure is the calculation of what is called the *safety distance for the protection of persons*, also known as the *site-specific safety distance*. This is the distance around an antenna within which the limits for the protection of persons are *not* complied with. It must be ensured that no unauthorised persons are in this area while transmitting. This is generally assumed to be the case if the area lies entirely on your own property, in what is called the *controllable area*.

In addition to the notification, which consists of a few forms, a *comprehensible* drawing of the site-specific safety distance and of the area controllable by the operator of the amateur station must be attached. The BNetzA provides instructions for preparing the notification for download.

Further technical documents must be prepared, e.g. *comprehensible* documentation of compliance with the requirements, antenna patterns, site plans, a construction drawing or sketch with dimensions. These, however, are not submitted with the notification, but are kept available at the amateur station and presented to the Federal Network Agency on request.

[question:VE513]
[question:VD107]

All the documents prepared must be checked regularly to make sure they are up to date. If something has been changed at the amateur radio installation so that it no longer corresponds to the previous circumstances, a new notification must be submitted. This applies, for example, when a further antenna mast is put up, or when a change of antenna or a higher transmit power makes a larger distance necessary than before.

Compliance with the limits and the resulting safety distance for persons must be demonstrated either by calculations or by field-strength measurements and documented in a comprehensible form. The calculation may be made using the simplified assessment procedure, by far-field or near-field calculation, or with the "[Watt Wächter](https://50ohm.de/ww)" software published by the Federal Network Agency. The calculation methods are described in a document that can be downloaded from the authority.

Radio amateurs sometimes transmit on several frequencies at the same time. When calculating the safety distance for the protection of persons, all simultaneous transmissions of a fixed amateur station must therefore be taken into account. If more than one antenna is used for transmitting at the same time, all the antennas must be considered together for the safety distance for the protection of persons. This is always the case when their safety distances overlap.
