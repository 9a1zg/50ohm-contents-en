We have already met various ways in which radio waves can propagate:
- direct wave: propagation in a straight line from the transmitter to the receiver (e.g. with line of sight)
- sky wave: propagation by refraction at areas with free electrons in the ionosphere
- ground wave: propagation along the earth's surface

The ground wave interacts with the ground and is thereby able to travel parallel to the earth's surface, that is, to follow the curvature of the earth. This works better the lower the frequency and the more conductive the ground; the wave must also be vertically polarised. In other words, the ground wave follows the curvature of the earth and goes beyond the geographical horizon. It is attenuated more in higher frequency ranges than in lower frequency ranges.

[question:EH212]

This form of propagation is therefore relevant above all on long and medium wave, where the range of the ground wave is several hundred kilometres – every station you hear there during the day is received via the ground wave. Towards higher frequencies it still reaches beyond the geographical horizon, but its range falls off rapidly, which is why on HF it is still of some importance only on the lower bands, e.g. on $\qty{160}{\meter}$.
