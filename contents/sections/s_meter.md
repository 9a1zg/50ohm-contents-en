In Class N we have already met the *S meter*, both in its analogue form (Fig. [ref:a_s_meter_analog]) and in its digital form (Fig. [ref:a_s_meter_digital]). It is used to show the strength of the RF signal present at the receiver input.

The scale of an S meter usually runs from S1 to S9. A change of one S unit corresponds to $\qty{6}{\dB}$. Stronger signals above S9 are no longer given in further S units, but in decibels above S9, for example as "S9 + $\qty{20}{\dB}$".

Since the decibel scale is logarithmic, an increase of $\qty{6}{\dB}$ corresponds to doubling the input voltage, or quadrupling the input power. Conversely, a reduction of $\qty{6}{\dB}$ corresponds to halving the voltage, or reducing the power to a quarter.

In the HF range up to $\qty{30}{\mega\hertz}$, an S reading of S9 corresponds to exactly $\qty{50}{\micro\volt}$ across $\qty{50}{\ohm}$.
From the VHF range ($\qty{144}{\mega\hertz}$) upwards, an S reading of S9 corresponds to exactly $\qty{5}{\micro\volt}$ across $\qty{50}{\ohm}$.

<tip>
The S meters of HF radios usually show only readings around S9 reasonably reliably, because they are often calibrated only at this one value. Lower S readings in particular are shown only very inaccurately. The logarithmic characteristic of an S meter is often interpolated only inadequately. By definition there is no S reading of S0, since the receiver's noise floor or internal noise is always present. If the S meter shows no reading at the lower end, the received signal is very weak, but it never has the value S0. This value should therefore not be reported either. 
</tip>
