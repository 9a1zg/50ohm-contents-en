
Delays occur in the transmission and processing of signals. The speed of light determines the time an electromagnetic wave takes to travel from the transmitter to the receiver. Processing signals causes further delays, above all through temporary storage in buffers and through filters. Unlike digital signal processing, analogue methods rarely cause noticeable delays, because as a rule there is no temporary storage and only comparatively simple filters are built.

The delay results in a propagation time between transmitter and receiver, or between the input and output of a processing stage. This propagation time is called latency and is measured in seconds. Typical latencies range from a few milliseconds to several seconds.
