## Balance

* Centre-fed dipoles are *balanced antennas*
* Have the same voltage with respect to earth at both poles (e.g. the feed points), apart from the sign
* The case with dipoles and the Yagi-Uda antennas based on them
* The ground-plane antenna is *unbalanced*, as it has earth potential at the point where the radials are connected

<note>
* Earth potential: voltage of zero with respect to earth
* Balanced and unbalanced cables come later, with the cables
</note>

## Loop antennas

* In the shape of a circle, square, triangle, …
* Popular: delta loop antenna in the shape of a delta (Δ), as only one mast is needed

## Magnetic loop

* Magnetic loop antenna, as radiation in the near field takes place via the magnetic field
* Circumference approx. $\frac{\lambda}{10}$
* Efficiency $\qtyrange{1}{10}{\percent}$ when transmitting
* Less affected by electrically conductive or attenuating objects in the near field

<note>
* Can be used indoors
* Even so, with the strong magnetic field, you should not sit right next to it
</note>

## Directional properties

* Shown as a *radiation pattern*
* For one plane, the gain, or the field strength or radiated power, is plotted for each direction
* The farther the curve is from the centre, the greater the gain, or the higher the field strength and radiated power in the far field
* The antenna is often shown in it as well

---
### Directional properties of a dipole

<left>
* Radiates at right angles to the wire
* Viewed in one plane, this gives lobes beside the dipole
* A vertical dipole radiates all round
</left>
