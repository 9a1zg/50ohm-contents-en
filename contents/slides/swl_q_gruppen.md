## Q codes

* consist of three letters and begin with *Q*
* were developed for fast telegraphy
* are now also used in voice operation
* with a question mark usually a question, without one a statement or a request

--- style="font-size: smaller;"

### Important Q codes

| l: Code | X: Meaning |
| QRM | I am being interfered with by other stations |
| QRN | I am troubled by static |
| QRO? | Shall I increase transmitter power? |
| QRT | Stop sending |
| QRV | I am ready |

--- style="font-size: smaller;"

### More important Q codes

| l: Code | X: Meaning |
| QRX? | When will you call me again? |
| QRZ? | Who is calling me? |
| QSB? | Are my signals fading? |
| QSL? | Can you acknowledge receipt? |
| QSO? | Can you communicate with ... direct? |
| QTH | My location is ... |

<note>
Memory aid: QRM is man-made interference, QRN natural interference, and with QSB the signal strength goes "bergauf und bergab" (German for "uphill and downhill").
</note>
