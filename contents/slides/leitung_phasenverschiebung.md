## Propagation and phase shift of electromagnetic waves  

* Electromagnetic waves propagate at the speed of light  
* In free space this is the speed of light in a vacuum; in a conductor the velocity factor must be taken into account  

---

* The time delay in conductors leads to a measurable phase shift ($\qty{0}{\degree}$ to $\qty{360}{\degree}$)  
* 360° corresponds to one complete period of the oscillation, i.e. $\qty{360}{\degree} = \qty{0}{\degree}$  
* The *electrical length* of a coaxial cable already includes the velocity factor  
* One wavelength ($\lambda$) corresponds to $\qty{360}{\degree}$; e.g. $\lambda/2$ corresponds to exactly $\frac{\qty{360}{\degree}}{2} = \qty{180}{\degree}$
