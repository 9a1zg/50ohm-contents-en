## Large and small values

* In amateur radio we have large and small values
* To save writing lots of 0s, we have already abbreviated with unit prefixes, e.g. with milli ($\unit{\milli}$) or kilo ($\unit{\kilo}$)
* More units will follow in the course
  * Voltage ($\unit{\volt}$), current ($\unit{\ampere}$), power ($\unit{\watt}$), resistance ($\unit{\ohm}$)

---

## Powers of ten

* Unit prefixes cannot be entered directly into most calculators
* Instead, the power of ten is used
* Kilo corresponds to $1000$ or $10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10$
  * Abbreviated $10^3$, spoken "10 to the power of 3" or "the 3rd power of 10"
* Rule of thumb: multiply by $10$ as many times as the exponent says
  * for numbers without a decimal point: add as many zeros as the exponent says

---

## Example

<fragment>
Kilo, $10^3$:
$\qty{1500}{\hertz} \rArr \qty{1.5}{\kilo\hertz} \rArr \qty{1.5e3}{\hertz}$
</fragment>

<fragment>
Mega, $10^6$:
$\qty{1500000}{\hertz} \rArr \qty{1.5}{\mega\hertz} \rArr \qty{1.5e6}{\hertz}$
</fragment>

---

* Milli corresponds to $\frac{1}{1000}$ or $\frac{1}{10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10}$
* Abbreviated $10^{-3}$

<fragment>
$\qty{0.0035}{\volt} \rArr \qty{3.5}{\milli\volt} \rArr \qty{3.5e-3}{\volt}$
$\qty{0.000022}{\volt} \rArr \qty{22}{\micro\volt} \rArr \qty{22e-6}{\volt}$
</fragment>

## Calculator

* Key *EXP* / *E* / *$\times 10^x$* = "times ten to the power of …"
* To type in $\num{3.5e6}$: $3.5 \rightarrow$ EXP $\rightarrow 6$
* *ENG* shifts the exponent by 3
* *S/D* often changes the representation
* exact keys: instruction manual
