<right>
* Ohmic losses in the metal add to the losses in feeders
* As the frequency rises, the current flows more and more at the surface of the conductor – an effect known as the *skin effect*
* The skin effect increases the ohmic losses
</right>
<note>
The *cause of the skin effect* is that high-frequency electromagnetic fields cannot penetrate deep into a metallic conductor, because they give rise to induced opposing currents. In ideal conductors the interior would remain free of field, and the current would flow only at the surface.
</note>
