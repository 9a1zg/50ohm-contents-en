## The RST system

The quality of the contact depends on
* transmit power
* the antenna used
* distance
* current propagation conditions

<fragment>
In the report, the receiving station describes the quality of the contact
</fragment>

<note>
* A signal report is a report on reception
</note>

### RST system

| l: Value | l: Range | l: Meaning | l: English |
| R | 1 - 5 | readability | Readability |
| S | 1 - 9 | signal strength | Signal Strength |
| T | 1 - 9 | tone quality | Tone |
[table:n_rst:The components of the RST report]

<note>
* Everything is explained in more detail on the next slides
* R: subjective
* S: read from the S meter
* T: only for Morse telegraphy and digital transmissions
</note>

--- style="font-size: 0.8em;"

#### Readability

Subjective assessment of readability (intelligibility)

| l: R | l: Assessment |
| 1 | unreadable |
| 2 | readable at times |
| 3| readable with difficulty |
| 4 | readable without difficulty |
| 5 | perfectly readable |
[table:n_rst_r:Guidance for the subjective assessment of readability (intelligibility)]

---

#### Signal Strength

Read from the radio

[photo:123:n_rst_s-meter:Display of an IC9700 transceiver; highlighted is the S meter, which shows the current received signal level]

<note>
* S from 1 to 9
* beyond that, +x dB
* e.g. 9+20dB
</note>

---

#### Tone

* Only used in Morse telegraphy and digital transmission methods
* Subjective assessment of the tone
* Left out in voice communication

--- style="font-size: 0.8em;"

in voice communication

| with difficulty (=3) | 3 | $\rightarrow$ | 33 |
| unintelligible (=1) | 3 | $\rightarrow$ | 13 |
