* Assigned most often
* There are three licence classes in Germany

1. Class N (Entry Level Licence)
2. Class E (Novice)
3. Class A (Advanced)

* The class can be recognised from the prefix and the digit
* See the [Call Sign Plan](https://50ohm.de/rzp)

<note>
* Other countries – but not all – have similar systems
</note>

## Licence

* After passing the exam, apply to the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA) for *authorisation to participate in the amateur service*
* You then receive the licence document with your personal call sign
* Only then may you start operating
* The licence is not transferable

<note>
* The licence document is now a PDF and may be sent by e-mail
* What counts is the entry in the call sign list/database
</note>

## Preferred call sign

* Preferred call signs can be given on the application
* Look for free call signs in the [BNetzA call sign list](https://50ohm.de/rzl) or with the [BNetzA online search](https://50ohm.de/rza)
* There is no entitlement to the assignment of a particular call sign
* Without a preferred call sign, the BNetzA chooses one

<note>
* Possibly give several call signs
* Call signs may have been assigned in the meantime
</note>

---
### Choosing a call sign

* Short form of your name
* Initials
* Good intelligibility when spoken
* Easy Morse telegraphy
* Wordplay

## Change or withdrawal of call signs

* As a rule, the BNetzA does not change a call sign
* It may become necessary when regulations or licence classes change
* When a new application for authorisation for the amateur service is made, a new call sign is issued

<note>
* Example: the change of GDR call signs
* Training call signs DN1-8 will be withdrawn in future
</note>
