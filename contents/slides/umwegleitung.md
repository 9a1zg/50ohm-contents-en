* $\lambda/2$ delay line as a further way of suppressing common-mode currents

### How the $\lambda/2$ delay line works
* Balanced antenna: both terminals need an RF voltage (equal in magnitude, $\qty{180}{\degree}$ out of phase)
* With the $\lambda/2$ delay line, one of the terminals (e.g. of a folded dipole) is connected to the inner conductor via a $\qty{180}{\degree}$ phase shift
* The screen of the coaxial cable is at earth potential – so no common-mode currents arise
* Mind the impedance: the antenna must have four times the impedance of the coaxial cable, as each terminal presents only half the resistance
