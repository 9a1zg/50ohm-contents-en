## Balanced twin feeders
* Twin feeders are fed and loaded in a balanced way
* At any given point, current and voltage are the same on both conductors – only with opposite sign

### Radiation and screening of feeders
* Antiphase currents largely cancel at a distance
* In free space there is nevertheless slight radiation
* In the near field of other lines the coupling becomes stronger
* So feeders inside the house should as a rule be screened (usually as coaxial cable)

## RF properties of coaxial cables
* Coaxial cables come in different designs
* The main ones considered are characteristic impedance, cable loss and velocity factor
* Mechanical properties such as bending radius and return loss are not among the electrical RF properties

### Effect of the dielectric
* The dielectric between the inner and outer conductors determines the velocity factor
* It affects the velocity of propagation, which is lower than the speed of light in free space
* Common materials are polyethylene (PE) and Teflon (PTFE)
* Foaming reduces the cable loss

### Velocity factor and electrical length
* Velocity factor: ratio of the mechanical length ($L_\mathrm{G}$) to the electrical length ($L_\mathrm{E}$)
* Formula: $k_\mathrm{v} = \frac{L_\mathrm{G}}{L_\mathrm{E}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_\mathrm{r}}}$
* For non-foamed polyethylene, $\epsilon_\mathrm{r} \approx 2.29$
