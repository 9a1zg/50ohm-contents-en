<right>
* Modulating signal is modulated onto the carrier by changing its amplitude
* Frequency of the carrier remains unchanged
* Changing the amplitude changes the shape of the carrier $\rightarrow$ no longer corresponds to a sine wave
* Additional frequencies are called *sidebands*
</right>
<note>
It is somewhat paradoxical that, with amplitude modulation, the frequency nevertheless appears to change. There is an explanation under "In depth" on the [50Ohm page](/N_am.html). Anyone who wants the details can look up the *trigonometric addition formulas* to see how the calculation is done.
</note>

---

<left>
* The sidebands contain the transmitted information, e.g. the speech
* The *bandwidth* occupied by AM is twice the highest frequency of the modulating signal
</left>
<right>
[picture:476:n_seitenband:Symbolic representation of an amplitude-modulated signal with carrier and sidebands]
</right>
<note>
* Example: with a highest frequency of 2700 Hz, the AM emission has a bandwidth of 5400 Hz.
</note>
