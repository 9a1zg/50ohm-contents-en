### Split operation in detail

* Split operation is the most widely used method of giving many callers a contact.
* In split operation, the sought-after station receives on a different frequency from the one on which it transmits.
* The station calling CQ announces split operation by giving the frequency or the frequency range on which it receives.
* The figures are always given in kHz.

<note>
One advantage of split operation is that the special station can still be received clearly and does not itself get "lost" in the pile-up.
</note>

---

* "5 up" means: the calling station is listening $\qty{5}{\kilo\hertz}$ above its own transmit frequency
* Example: if the station transmits on $\qty{28410}{\kilo\hertz}$, you must reply on $\qty{28415}{\kilo\hertz}$

* More often, a whole frequency range is given, e.g. "split up 28420 to 28430" or in short "tuning 420 to 430 up"
* Meaning: the station receives on varying frequencies within the range given
* Since nobody knows which frequency the station is listening on at any given moment, you have to pick a frequency from the range at random and reply there

[picture:673:n_split_verkehr_anrufbereich:Split operation, transmit frequency of the sought-after station and the frequency range for replies in which it receives]

<note>
Advantage for the station calling CQ: only a small proportion of the interested radio amateurs hit the right frequency, so it can pick out individual call signs more easily.
</note>
