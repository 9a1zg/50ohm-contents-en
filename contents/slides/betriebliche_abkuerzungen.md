* Many abbreviations come from Morse telegraphy, as they are quicker to send than words written out in full
* Many abbreviations are laid down in the Radio Regulations (RR)
  * Operating abbreviations for Morse telegraphy
  * Q codes (next section)
* Long since common in telephony too, and in everyday language among radio amateurs
* Often derived from English terms

--- style="font-size: 0.7em;"

| c: Abbreviation | X: Meaning |
| BK | Interruption of the transmission; informal handover (*B*rea*k*) |
| CQ | General call (from "seek you") |
| CW | *C*ontinuous *W*ave (synonym for Morse telegraphy) |
| K | Invitation to transmit ("*K*ommen", German for "over") |
| PSE | *P*lea*se* |
| R | Received (acknowledgement of receipt, *R*oger) |
| RX | Receiver |
| TX | Transmitter |
| TRX | Transceiver |
