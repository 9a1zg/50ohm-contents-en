## Wavelength

* The distance between two successive repetitions of a wave is called the *wavelength*
* The higher the frequency, the shorter the wavelength

<fragment>
The wavelength is denoted by the Greek letter $\lambda$ (lambda) and measured in metres ($\unit{\meter}$).
</fragment>

## Relationship between frequency and wavelength

* Via the speed of light
* A wave with a frequency of $\qty{1}{\hertz}$ travels $\qty{300000}{\kilo\meter}$ before the next cycle begins
* At $\qty{1000}{\hertz}$ it is only $\qty{300}{\kilo\meter}$
* At $\qty{1}{\mega\hertz}$ it is $\qty{300}{\meter}$

<note>
Propagation at the speed of light applies only in a vacuum
</note>

---
$f[\unit{\mega\hertz}] = \dfrac{300}{\lambda[\unit{\meter}]} \quad\quad\quad \lambda[\unit{\meter}] = \dfrac{300}{f[\unit{\mega\hertz}]}$

<note>
The formulas are on the formula sheet
</note>
---
### Examples

Wavelength for the frequency $\qty{145.3}{\mega\hertz}$

$\lambda[\unit{\meter}] = \dfrac{300}{f[\unit{\mega\hertz}]} = \dfrac{300}{\qty{145.3}{\mega\hertz}} \approx \qty{2.06}{\meter}$

<fragment>
Frequency for the wavelength $\qty{2.06}{\meter}$

$f[\unit{\mega\hertz}] = \dfrac{300}{\lambda[\unit{\meter}]} = \dfrac{300}{\qty{2.06}{\meter}} \approx \qty{145.3}{\mega\hertz}$
</fragment>
