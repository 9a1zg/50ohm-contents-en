## How a vector network analyser works  

* Generates a *variable-frequency* signal  
* Signal is applied (e.g. to an antenna or tuned circuit)  
* Measures how the signal is changed (e.g. by reflection)  
* Records both amplitude and phase  
* Allows the resistive and reactive parts and the standing wave ratio to be determined

### VNA display – splitting the impedance  

* Split into resistive ($R$) and reactive ($X$) parts  
* Resistance often in $\Omega$, reactance sometimes as $j\Omega$  
* Instruments do not all display impedance values in the same way  
* In electrical engineering the $j$ stands for the imaginary unit ($i$)  
* Positive reactances indicate inductive behaviour, negative ones capacitive behaviour  

<note>
*Imaginary numbers* are a well-proven tool in electrical engineering and mathematics.<br/>  
- To solve $x^2 = -1$, the imaginary number $i$ is defined, with $i^2 = -1$.  <br/>
- Real numbers cannot do this, since the product of two negative numbers is positive.  <br/>
- Complex numbers such as $54 - 12i$ combine real and imaginary parts.  <br/>
- In electrical engineering $j$ is used instead of $i$ to avoid confusion with current.
</note>
