## Period

<left>
* Duration of one complete oscillation
* Needed to determine the frequency, e.g. on an oscilloscope
</left>
<right>
[picture:790:e_periode_amplitude:Period and amplitude of a sine wave]
</right>

---

* The period is inversely proportional to the frequency
* Symbol T, unit second (s)

<fragment>
$T = \dfrac{1}{f} \Rightarrow f = \dfrac{1}{T}$
</fragment>

## Reading off the period

<left>
* Count the squares of a whole period, starting at a zero crossing
* Multiply by the time per square
* With 8 squares and $\qty{2}{\milli\second}$ per square $\rightarrow 8 \cdot \qty{2}{\milli\second} = \qty{16}{\milli\second}$
</left>

## Determining the frequency

$f = \dfrac{1}{T}$

First determine the period, then calculate the frequency

---
[question:EB410]
<note>
* Solution on the next slide
</note>
---
### Solution

One period is 4 squares long
$T = 4 \cdot \qty{5}{\milli\second} = \qty{20}{\milli\second}$
$f = \dfrac{1}{T} = \dfrac{1}{\qty{20e-3}{\second}} = $
$0.05 \cdot \frac{1}{\qty{10^{-3}}{\second}} = 0.05 \cdot \qty{10^3}{\hertz} = \qty{0.05}{\kilo\hertz} = \qty{50}{\hertz}$

## AF distortion
<left>
* Sometimes signals are distorted unintentionally.
* This happens, for example, when too high an input voltage is fed into an amplifier.
* The amplifier is then said to be overdriven, and its output signal distorted.
* This can be made visible with an oscilloscope.
</left>
