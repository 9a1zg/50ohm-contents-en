<left>
[picture:867:a_langer_kurzer_weg:Long and short path on a sphere]
A straight-line connection between two places on a sphere always runs along the great circle
</left>
<right>
* With a directional antenna, the heading of the direction of maximum radiation decides which destination can be reached
* Another place can therefore be reached with two different headings
* The two paths differ in length
</right>

---

<left>
* The two headings differ by $\qty{180}{\degree}$
* Example: from Berlin to Sydney/Australia the short path is at $\qty{75}{\degree}$, the long path at $\qty{255}{\degree}$
</left>
<right>
[picture:1000:a_beam_karte:Great-circle map with Berlin as the location of the amateur station]
</right>

---

<left>
* Great-circle maps can look different for other starting points
* Example: the view from Sydney/Australia
</left>
<right>

</right>

### Calculation

For the long path
* For a beam heading between $\qty{0}{\degree}$ and $\qty{180}{\degree}$: beam heading + $\qty{180}{\degree}$
* For a beam heading between $\qty{180}{\degree}$ and $\qty{360}{\degree}$: beam heading - $\qty{180}{\degree}$

<note>
Edge cases for know-alls: $\qty{0}{\degree}$, $\qty{180}{\degree}$ and $\qty{360}{\degree}$ can be calculated with either formula
</note>
