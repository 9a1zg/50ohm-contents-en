### Violations and consequences

* Violations of the Amateur Radio Act (Amateurfunkgesetz, AFuG) or the Amateur Radio Ordinance (Amateurfunkverordnung, AFuV) are not always punished with fines straight away
* Radio amateurs are hit harder when restrictions of operation or even a ban on operation are imposed on them

---

* In the event of continued violations of the AFuG and the AFuV, the licence can even be revoked - the sharpest weapon of the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur, BNetzA)
* Even after the licence has been revoked, you may still call yourself a radio amateur, but you are excluded from amateur radio operation
* There is no provision for taking away the amateur radio certificate itself - anyone who has passed the examination remains a radio amateur

Administrative offences within the meaning of the Amateur Radio Act (AFuG), Section 9, are:

* Operation without a licence and therefore without a call sign
* Providing telecommunications services on a commercial basis
* Passing messages for and to third parties

<fragment>
The Federal Network Agency can punish a violation with a fine.

* An administrative offence under the Telecommunications Act (Telekommunikationsgesetz, TKG) is the use of frequencies without a frequency assignment
* If a radio amateur transmits with an amateur radio installation outside the assigned amateur bands, the frequency is being used without an assignment and an administrative offence is committed
