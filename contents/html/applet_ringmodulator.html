The following applet lets you look at the two switching states of the ring modulator separately. The threshold voltages of the diodes are neglected, and the transformers are assumed to have a ratio of 1:1. In addition, a square-wave voltage is assumed for $U_2$. As a result, the switching is hard. An output filter must later filter out the high frequencies this produces.

The output voltage is therefore:

$ U_3 = \left(+1\,\mathrm{V}+\frac{U_1}{2}\right) - \left(+1\,\mathrm{V}-\frac{U_1}{2}\right) = U_1 $

The multiplication of $U_1$ and $U_2$ works!
The output voltage is therefore:

$ U_3 = \left(-1\,\mathrm{V}-\frac{U_1}{2}\right) - \left(-1\,\mathrm{V}+\frac{U_1}{2}\right) = -U_1 $ \]

The multiplication of $U_1$ and $U_2$ works!
