At $\qty{3.6}{\mega\hertz}$ the wavelength is approximately

$\lambda=\frac{\qty{300000}{\kilo\meter\per\second}}{\qty{3.6}{\mega\hertz}} \approx\qty{83.3}{\meter}.$

A half-wave dipole would therefore ideally have to be approximately

$\frac{\lambda}{2}\approx\qty{41.7}{\meter}$

long. However, the dipole described in the following question has only twice $\qty{15}{\meter}$, that is, a total length of $\qty{30}{\meter}$. It is therefore mechanically too short for $\qty{3.6}{\mega\hertz}$.

To bring it to resonance at this frequency, the two halves of the dipole must therefore be lengthened electrically. To do this, a coil is inserted into each leg.
