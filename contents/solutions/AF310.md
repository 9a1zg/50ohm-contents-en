The circuit diagram shows a tuned circuit in which part
of the capacitance of the tuned circuit is formed by a varactor diode.

The capacitance of the varactor diode depends on the voltage applied
across it. A DC voltage is first applied to the varactor diode through the resistor,
and this gives the varactor diode a
particular capacitance.

If an audio signal is fed in through the choke and the coupling
capacitor on the left-hand side, this audio signal is superimposed
on the DC voltage fed in through the resistor,
i.e. the voltage across the varactor diode varies with the amplitude
of the input signal.

As the voltage changes, the capacitance of the
varactor diode changes, and as the capacitance changes, the
resonant frequency of the tuned circuit changes.

The tuned circuit is the frequency-determining part of the oscillator circuit
that follows, i.e. the change in amplitude of the
input signal becomes a change in the output frequency of the
oscillator, and that is frequency modulation.
