## Explanation

If the electrical length of the connection between the transmitter and the equipotential bonding, as a line, is about $\lambda/4$ (or an odd multiple of it), it can behave as a lossy RF transformer section.

A quarter-wave line transforms an impedance, so that a low terminating resistance at one end can appear as a high resistance at the other end.

In this specific case, this means:

* At low frequencies, the equipotential bonding is a low-resistance reference point.
* At radio frequencies, this low-resistance point can be transformed in impedance by the $\lambda/4$ line.
* The line can thereby act as a resonator, and standing waves form.
* A voltage maximum (RF voltage antinode) can form at the transmitter end.
* As a result, a noticeable RF voltage is present on the transmitter case ("hot case"), although the case is correctly earthed for DC and the mains frequency.
