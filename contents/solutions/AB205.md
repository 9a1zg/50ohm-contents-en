The voltage source has an internal resistance $R_i$, across which a voltage is dropped under load.

First we calculate the current through the load resistor:

$ I = \frac{U}{R} = \frac{\qty{4.8}{\volt}}{\qty{1.2}{\ohm}} = \qty{4}{\ampere} $

The voltage has fallen from $\qty{5.0}{\volt}$ to $\qty{4.8}{\volt}$.  
So the voltage dropped across the internal resistance is:

$ U_i = \qty{5.0}{\volt} - \qty{4.8}{\volt} = \qty{0.2}{\volt} $

Now the internal resistance can be calculated:

$ R_i = \frac{U_i}{I} = \frac{\qty{0.2}{\volt}}{\qty{4}{\ampere}} = \qty{0.05}{\ohm} $
