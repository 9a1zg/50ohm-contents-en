Since field strengths are being compared here (they contain a voltage, after all!), the ratio in decibels is calculated with the factor $\num{20}$.

For the gain of the beam, the field strength in the forward direction is compared with the field strength of the half-wave dipole:

$20 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{\qty{300}{\micro\volt\per\meter}}{\qty{128}{\micro\volt\per\meter}}\right) \approx \qty{7.4}{\decibel}$.

The beam therefore has a gain of about $\qty{7.4}{\decibel}$ over the half-wave dipole, that is, $\qty{7.4}{\dBd}$.

For the front-to-back ratio, the field strengths of the beam in the forward and backward directions are compared:

$20 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{\qty{300}{\micro\volt\per\meter}}{\qty{20}{\micro\volt\per\meter}}\right) \approx \qty{23.5}{\decibel}$.

The beam therefore has a gain of about $\qty{7.4}{\dBd}$ and a front-to-back ratio of about $\qty{23.5}{\decibel}$.
