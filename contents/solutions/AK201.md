## Explanation

A high-value discharge resistor is used for the controlled discharge of power supply capacitors. It limits the discharge current and so reduces the risk of arcing, damage to components and electrical hazards.

### Why a high-value resistor?

When a capacitor is discharged, the current flows through the discharge resistor $R$. It is chosen so that:

* the initial current remains limited:

    $I_0 = \frac{U_0}{R}$

* no dangerously high current peaks occur,
* no arcing occurs,
* components and PCB tracks are protected from overload,
* the discharge nevertheless takes place within a reasonable time in practice.

A low-value resistor or a short circuit, on the other hand, would lead to:

* very high initial currents,
* possible damage to the capacitor and PCB tracks,
* uncontrolled current and voltage peaks,
* an increased risk of fire and injury.

### Why must the power rating of the resistor be adequate?

The energy stored in the capacitor is:

$E = \frac{1}{2} \cdot C \cdot {U_0}^2$

During discharge, all of it is converted into heat in the resistor.

The initial power dissipation is:

$P_0 = \frac{{U_0}^2}{R}$

For $R = \qty{100}{\kilo\Ohm}$ and $U_0 = \qty{400}{\volt}$, this gives a power of $P_0 = \qty{1.6}{\watt}$.

The resistor must therefore:

* have an adequate power rating,
* be able to withstand short-term overload (pulse operation),
* be dimensioned with a thermal safety margin, to avoid overheating.
