For the bandwidth of a frequency-modulated signal, Carson's rule can be used:

$B\approx2 \cdot \left(\Delta f_{\textrm{T}} + f_{\textrm{mod max}} \right)$

Here $\Delta f_{\textrm{T}} = \qty{1.8}{\kilo\hertz}$ is the frequency deviation and $f_{\textrm{mod max}} = \qty{2}{\kilo\hertz}$ the highest modulating frequency.

Substituting gives:

$B \approx 2 \cdot \left(\qty{1.8}{\kilo\hertz} + \qty{2}{\kilo\hertz}\right) = 2 \cdot \qty{3.8}{\kilo\hertz} = \qty{7.6}{\kilo\hertz}$

The bandwidth of the FM signal is therefore approximately $\qty{7.6}{\kilo\hertz}$. The carrier frequency of $\qty{145}{\mega\hertz}$ is not needed for this calculation.
