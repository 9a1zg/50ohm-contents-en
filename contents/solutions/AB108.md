At the boundary layer of a PN junction, free electrons diffuse from the n-type region into the p-type region. There they recombine with the holes present.

This partly evens out the excess of electrons on the n-side and the deficiency of electrons on the p-side. Fixed charges remain behind in the boundary layer, so that a depletion layer with few charge carriers forms. It acts as an insulator and at first prevents any further transport of charge.
