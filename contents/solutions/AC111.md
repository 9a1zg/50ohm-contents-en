We are given a capacitor to which a sinusoidal AC signal is applied. In the steady state, however, an ideal capacitor absorbs no real power, because current and voltage are $\qty{90}{\degree}$ out of phase.

The energy is merely stored periodically in the electric field of the capacitor and then returned to the source. The mean value of the power over a whole period is therefore zero.

Since a real capacitor always has parasitic losses (e.g. in the form of resistances), a small amount of real power is absorbed. The real power absorbed is therefore approximately $P = \qty{0}{\watt}$
