In PLL frequency synthesis, the output frequency of the VCO is divided down in the feedback path by the divider *:n* and compared in the phase detector $\varphi$ with the reference frequency at point A.

The control loop ensures that the two input frequencies at the phase detector are equal:

$f_A = \frac{f_{VCO}}{n}$

or

$f_{VCO} = n \cdot f_A$

The division ratio *n* is set via inputs B and C and is always a *whole number*. If *n* is increased by exactly 1, the output frequency jumps by exactly $f_A$:

$\Delta f_{VCO} = (n+1) \cdot f_A - n \cdot f_A = f_A$

The smallest possible frequency step at the output – i.e. the *channel spacing* – is therefore equal to the reference frequency at point A.

Since a channel spacing of $\qty{12.5}{\kilo\hertz}$ is required, the frequency

$f_A = \qty{12.5}{\kilo\hertz}$

must also be present at point A.
