The oscilloscope is set to $\qty{3}{\volt}$ per division.

In the diagram, the maximum peak voltage is about two divisions:

$ \hat{U}_\mathrm{gemessen} = 2 \cdot \qty{3}{\volt} = \qty{6}{\volt} $

The measurement was made with a $\num{10{:}1}$ probe. The actual peak voltage is therefore ten times as high:

$ \hat{U} = 10 \cdot \qty{6}{\volt} = \qty{60}{\volt} $

The RMS voltage is therefore:

$ U_\mathrm{eff} = \frac{\hat{U}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\qty{60}{\volt}}{\sqrt{2}} \approx \qty{42.42}{\volt} $

The PEP is calculated from the RMS voltage across the $\qty{50}{\ohm}$ load:

$ P_\mathrm{PEP} = \frac{{U_\mathrm{eff}}^2}{R} $

Substituting the values:

$ P_\mathrm{PEP} = \frac{(\qty{42.42}{\volt})^2}{\qty{50}{\ohm}} \approx \qty{36}{\watt} $

The output power of the transmitter is therefore:

$ P_\mathrm{PEP} = \qty{36}{\watt} $
