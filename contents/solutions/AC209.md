In a real inductor, losses occur besides the inductive reactance $X_L$. These losses can be combined mathematically into a series loss resistance $R_S$.

The Q factor $Q$ describes how small these losses are in relation to the reactance:

$Q = \frac{X_L}{R_S}$

The greater the losses, the greater $R_S$ and the smaller the Q factor $Q$. The dissipation factor $\tan\delta$ describes the same relationship the other way round and is the reciprocal of the Q factor:

$\tan\delta = \frac{1}{Q} =\frac{R_S}{X_L}$
