In each branch, four resistors of $\qty{150}{\ohm}$ each are connected in series. The resistance of one branch is therefore:

$R_\mathrm{Zweig} = 4 \cdot \qty{150}{\ohm} = \qty{600}{\ohm}$

For the parallel connection to give a total resistance of approximately $\qty{50}{\ohm}$, the number of branches needed is:

$n = \frac{R_\mathrm{Zweig}}{R_\mathrm{ges}} = \frac{\qty{600}{\ohm}}{\qty{50}{\ohm}} = 12$

So $\num{12}$ parallel branches of four resistors each are needed. In total, that is:

$N = 12 \cdot 4 = 48$

Since each resistor may be loaded with $\qty{1}{\watt}$ and the power is shared equally between identical branches, the total permitted continuous power is:

$P_\mathrm{ges} = 48 \cdot \qty{1}{\watt} = \qty{48}{\watt}$

The dummy load therefore needs $\num{48}$ resistors, and it can handle a continuous power of about $\qty{48}{\watt}$.
