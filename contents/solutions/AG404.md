If the antenna is disconnected, there is an open circuit at the end of the line. The RF power arriving there is completely reflected.

The line has a loss of $\qty{5}{\decibel}$ on the way there and another $\qty{5}{\decibel}$ on the way back. Over its whole path, the reflected wave is therefore attenuated by

$\qty{5}{\decibel}+\qty{5}{\decibel}=\qty{10}{\decibel}$.

An attenuation of $\qty{10}{\decibel}$ corresponds to a power ratio of $\num{10}$. Of the $\qty{10}{\watt}$ fed in, therefore,

$P_\mathrm{R}=\frac{\qty{10}{\watt}}{10}=\qty{1}{\watt}$

still arrives back at the line input as reflected power.

The forward power at the line input is $\qty{10}{\watt}$. Since power is proportional to the square of the voltage, the ratio of the reflected to the forward voltage is

$\frac{U_\mathrm{R}}{U_\mathrm{V}} =\sqrt{\frac{\qty{1}{\watt}}{\qty{10}{\watt}}} \approx\num{0.316}.$ 

The standing wave ratio is therefore

$s= \frac{U_\mathrm{V}+U_\mathrm{R}} {U_\mathrm{V}-U_\mathrm{R}} = \frac{1+\num{0.316}}{1-\num{0.316}} \approx\num{1.92}.$

So despite the open circuit at the end of the line, an SWR of only about $\num{1.92}$ is measured at the line input.
