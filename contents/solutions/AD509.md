An audio signal is produced by the microphone. The box with the triangle
represents an amplifier. From the label
"$\qtyrange{0.3}{3.4}{\kilo\hertz}$" it can be seen that this
amplifier is selective, i.e. it has what amounts to a built-in band-pass filter,
and amplifies only the frequency range between $\qty{300}{\hertz}$ and
$\qty{3.4}{\kilo\hertz}$, i.e. audio.

The two diodes connected in inverse parallel act as "hard"
amplitude limiting for the output signal of the amplifier. As long as
the amplitude of the signal is below the forward voltage of the diode
concerned, the diode does not conduct. If the amplitude of the signal
rises above the forward voltage of the diode, however, the diode starts to conduct
once the forward voltage is reached and so limits the signal level to
the forward voltage (for a silicon diode this would be about
$\qty{0.7}{\volt}$). With the two diodes connected in inverse parallel,
this happens for both half cycles – one diode clips
the positive half cycle, the other diode the negative half cycle.

The potentiometer acts as an adjustable potential divider, i.e. it
allows the amplitude of the audio signal to be set between $\qty{0}{\volt}$
and the maximum that results from the forward voltage of the
diodes.

The $\qty{3}{\kilo\hertz}$ low-pass filter on the far right filters out
frequency components above $\qty{3}{\kilo\hertz}$.

The bandwidth that matters for an FM signal (the so-called
"Carson bandwidth") is affected by two components: the
FM deviation, which in turn depends directly on the amplitude of the audio signal,
and the highest frequency in the audio signal.

Since the permitted bandwidth of a transmitted signal is limited by law,
it must be ensured that a transmitter cannot
exceed it. This circuit limits both
components:

* the diodes limit the amplitude of the audio signal and so also
  the FM deviation

* the low-pass filter limits the highest frequency in the signal

The correct answer to the question is therefore "Deviation limiting and
deviation setting in FM transceivers". The deviation is limited by
the diodes and set by the potentiometer.
