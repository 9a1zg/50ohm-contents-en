This circuit works as a voltage doubler, or peak-to-peak detector. The positive and the negative peak of the RF signal are captured, so that approximately the peak-to-peak voltage, minus the forward voltages of both diodes, is available at the output.

$U_{\mathrm{SS}} = \qty{15.3}{\volt} + 2 \cdot \qty{0.23}{\volt} = \qty{15.76}{\volt}$

From

$U_{\mathrm{SS}} = 2 \cdot \hat{U}$

it follows for the peak value of the RF voltage at the $\qty{5}{\ohm}$ tap:

$\hat{U} = \frac{U_{\mathrm{SS}}}{2} = \qty{7.88}{\volt}$

The voltage is measured at a $\qty{5}{\ohm}$ tap on the dummy load, which is $\qty{50}{\ohm}$ in total. Since the same current flows through the whole dummy load, the voltages are in the same ratio as the resistances. The RF peak voltage across the whole dummy load is therefore:

$\hat{U}_{\mathrm{Sender}} = \frac{\qty{50}{\ohm}}{\qty{5}{\ohm}} \cdot \hat{U} = 10 \cdot \qty{7.88}{\volt} = \qty{78.8}{\volt}$

With

$\hat{U} = U_{\mathrm{eff}} \cdot \sqrt{2}$

and

$P = \frac{U_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}{R}$

this gives:

$P = \frac{\hat{U}_{\mathrm{Sender}}^2}{2R} = \frac{(\qty{78.8}{\volt})^2}{2 \cdot \qty{50}{\ohm}} \approx \qty{62}{\watt}$

The RF power of the transmitter is therefore about $\qty{60}{\watt}$.
