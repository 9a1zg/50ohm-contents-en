Energy has the unit $\unit{\joule}$ (for joule).  
One joule ($\qty{1}{\joule}$) corresponds to one watt ($\qty{1}{\watt}$) acting for a duration of one second ($\qty{1}{\second}$): $\qty{1}{\joule} = \qty{1}{\watt\second}$.  
So $\qty{1}{\watt\hour} = \qty{3600}{\joule}$.

Tip: think of your electricity bill, where electricity consumption is usually also given in kWh ($\qty{1}{\kilo\watt\hour} = \qty{1000\cdot3600}{\joule}$).  

By elimination: the volt is a voltage, the watt is a power (i.e. energy per unit time).
