## Explanation:

In the far field, the magnitudes of the electric field strength $E$ and the magnetic field strength $H$ of an electromagnetic field are linked by the wave impedance $Z$.

$E = H \cdot Z$

In other words:

In the far field, the ratio of the electric field strength $E$ to the magnetic field strength $H$ is constant and equal to the wave impedance $Z$:

$\frac{E}{H} = Z$

This makes it possible to calculate one field component from a measurement of the other. In the near field this is not generally possible.

The wave impedance $Z$ depends on the medium in which the electromagnetic field propagates. 
For the impedance of free space $Z_0$ we have:

$Z_0  = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_0}{\varepsilon_0}} \approx \qty{120\pi}{\ohm} \approx \qty{377}{\ohm}$ 

Here $\mu_0$ and $\varepsilon_0$ are the permeability and the permittivity of a vacuum respectively. The wave impedance of air is practically identical to that of a vacuum.
