First we look at the input resistance of the circuit. In amateur radio this should be exactly $\qty{50}{\ohm}$. So this part of the calculation can really be skipped. For completeness, we nevertheless show that the total here comes to $\qty{50}{\ohm}$. 

The potential divider made up of the two $\qty{330}{\ohm}$ resistors has a total of:

$R_\mathrm{T}=\qty{330}{\ohm}+\qty{330}{\ohm}=\qty{660}{\ohm}$

This is in parallel with $R_1=\qty{54.1}{\ohm}$:

$R_\mathrm{in}=R_1\parallel R_\mathrm{T}$

$R_\mathrm{in}=\frac{\qty{54.1}{\ohm}\cdot\qty{660}{\ohm}}{\qty{54.1}{\ohm}+\qty{660}{\ohm}}\approx\qty{50}{\ohm}$

The circuit therefore forms an approximately $\qty{50}{\ohm}$ termination.

A DC voltage of $\qty{14.9}{\volt}$ is measured at the output. Because of the forward voltage of the silicon diode, $\qty{0.7}{\volt}$, the RF peak value at the input of the diode must be larger by this amount:

$\hat U_\mathrm{D}=\qty{14.9}{\volt}+\qty{0.7}{\volt}=\qty{15.6}{\volt}$

The diode is connected to the midpoint of the potential divider made up of the two equal $\qty{330}{\ohm}$ resistors. Only half the RF input voltage is therefore present there. The peak value at the input is therefore:

$\hat U_\mathrm{in}=2\cdot\qty{15.6}{\volt}=\qty{31.2}{\volt}$

For the power calculation we need the RMS value:

$U_\mathrm{eff}=\frac{\hat U_\mathrm{in}}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\qty{31.2}{\volt}}{\sqrt{2}}\approx\qty{22.1}{\volt}$

This gives the RF power:

$P=\frac{U_\mathrm{eff}^2}{R_\mathrm{in}}=\frac{(\qty{22.1}{\volt})^2}{\qty{50}{\ohm}}\approx\qty{9.7}{\watt}$

The RF input power is therefore about $\qty{9.7}{\watt}$.
