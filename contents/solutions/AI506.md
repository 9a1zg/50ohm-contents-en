# Given
* Measured frequency: $f_\text{Anzeige} = \qty{29}{\mega\hertz}$
* Accuracy: $\qty{0.01}{\percent}$

# Method 1
$\Delta f = \qty{29}{\mega\hertz} \cdot \qty{0.01}{\percent} = \qty{2900}{\hertz}$

# Method 2
(Without a calculator)  

$\qty{1}{\percent}$ corresponds to $1 \cdot {10^{-2}}$;  
$\qty{0.01}{\percent}$ corresponds to $1 \cdot {10^{-4}}$.  
So that the exponent ($10^6$) of $\unit{\mega\hertz}$ can easily be cancelled later, we "move the decimal point":  
$1 \cdot {10^{-4}} \rightarrow 100 \cdot {10^{-6}}$.

$\Delta f = \qty{29}{\mega\hertz} \cdot \qty{0.01}{\percent} = \qty{29 \cdot \cancel{10^6}}{\hertz} \cdot 100 \cdot \cancel{10^{-6}} = \qty{2900}{\hertz}$
