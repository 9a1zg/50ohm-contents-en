With a $\qty{50}{\ohm}$ dummy load, the SWR at the line input is about $\num{1}$. This tells us, to begin with, that the antenna feeder itself is in order.

With the antenna connected, on the other hand, an SWR of $\num{3}$ is measured. With the voltage of the forward wave $U_\mathrm{V}$ and of the reflected wave $U_\mathrm{R}$, the SWR is:

$s=\frac{U_\mathrm{V}+U_\mathrm{R}}{U_\mathrm{V}-U_\mathrm{R}}.$

For $s=\num{3}$ it follows that the voltage of the reflected wave is half that of the forward wave:

$\frac{U_\mathrm{R}}{U_\mathrm{V}}=\frac{3-1}{3+1}=\num{0.5}.$

Since power is proportional to the square of the voltage, the reflected power at the line input is therefore

$\num{0.5}^2=\num{0.25}$

of the forward power. Of the $\qty{10}{\watt}$ fed in, about

$\qty{10}{\watt}\cdot\num{0.25}=\qty{2.5}{\watt}$

therefore arrives back at the line input as reflected power.

The line has a loss of $\qty{3}{\decibel}$ in each direction. Of the $\qty{10}{\watt}$ fed in, therefore, only about $\qty{5}{\watt}$ reaches the antenna. On the way back, the reflected power is halved again.

For $\qty{2.5}{\watt}$ of reflected power still to arrive at the line input, about $\qty{5}{\watt}$ must have been reflected at the antenna. This is practically all the power arriving there.

The antenna therefore takes up practically no RF power and is faulty.
