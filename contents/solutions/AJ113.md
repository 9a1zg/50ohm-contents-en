In Germany, DVB-T2 is broadcast on frequencies between
$\qty{470}{\mega\hertz}$ and $\qty{694}{\mega\hertz}$.
If a $\qty{144}{\mega\hertz}$ transmitter overloads a DVB-T2 receiver with a
passive antenna, and the $\qty{2}{\meter}$ transmitter has harmonic
suppression that complies with the standards, that is, it radiates
significant transmit power only in the $\qty{2}{\meter}$ band,
it can be assumed that the input stage of the DVB-T2 receiver is so
broadband that not only signals in the DVB-T2 range but
also signals on lower frequencies, such as the
$\qty{2}{\meter}$ amateur band, get into the receiver.

To prevent signals of the
$\qty{2}{\meter}$ amateur band from reaching the receiver, a
high-pass filter can be connected ahead of the receiver, with its
cut-off frequency a little below the DVB-T2 range (and thus far above
the $\qty{2}{\meter}$ amateur band) – a little below, and
not exactly at, the lower end of the DVB-T2 range, because
the high-pass filter has only a limited skirt steepness, and
otherwise television signals at the lower edge of the DVB-T2 range would also be
attenuated too much.
