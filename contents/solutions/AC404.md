A varactor diode (varicap) is operated in the reverse direction. The depletion layer forms the effective capacitance.

If the applied voltage rises, the depletion layer becomes wider and the capacitance smaller. 

If the reverse voltage falls, the depletion layer becomes narrower and the capacitance larger (correct answer).

This can be explained by the formula for the capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor: $C = \varepsilon_0 \cdot \varepsilon_r \cdot \frac{A}{d}$, where $d$ is the thickness of the depletion layer. The larger $d$, the smaller the capacitance $C$.
