The frequency range $\qtyrange{436}{440}{\mega\hertz}$ has a bandwidth of $\qty{4}{\mega\hertz}$. However, since the receiver connected processes only the range $\qtyrange{28}{30}{\mega\hertz}$, and so a bandwidth of $\qty{2}{\mega\hertz}$, the desired receive range must be split into two sub-bands:

* Sub-band 1: $\qtyrange{436}{438}{\mega\hertz}$
* Sub-band 2: $\qtyrange{438}{440}{\mega\hertz}$

Since the oscillator frequency is to lie below the wanted signal, the following applies to the mixing:

$f_\mathrm{ZF} = f_\mathrm{HF} - f_\mathrm{OSZ}$

For the first sub-band, from $\qtyrange{436}{438}{\mega\hertz}$, this gives:

$f_\mathrm{OSZ} = \qty{436}{\mega\hertz} - \qty{28}{\mega\hertz} = \qty{408}{\mega\hertz}$

At the upper band edge we get the same:

$f_\mathrm{OSZ} = \qty{438}{\mega\hertz} - \qty{30}{\mega\hertz} = \qty{408}{\mega\hertz}$

For the second sub-band, from $\qtyrange{438}{440}{\mega\hertz}$, this gives correspondingly:

$f_\mathrm{OSZ} = \qty{438}{\mega\hertz} - \qty{28}{\mega\hertz} = \qty{410}{\mega\hertz}$

At the upper band edge we get the same:

$f_\mathrm{OSZ} = \qty{440}{\mega\hertz} - \qty{30}{\mega\hertz} = \qty{410}{\mega\hertz}$

The oscillator frequencies needed are therefore $\qty{408}{\mega\hertz}$ and $\qty{410}{\mega\hertz}$.

Since these frequencies are generated by multiplying the crystal oscillator frequency by nine, they must be divided by a factor of $\num{9}$:

$f_\mathrm{Quarz,1} = \frac{\qty{408}{\mega\hertz}}{9} = \qty{45.333}{\mega\hertz}$

$f_\mathrm{Quarz,2} = \frac{\qty{410}{\mega\hertz}}{9} \approx \qty{45.556}{\mega\hertz}$

The crystal oscillator must therefore be switchable between $\qty{45.333}{\mega\hertz}$ and $\qty{45.556}{\mega\hertz}$.
