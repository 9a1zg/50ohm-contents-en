The total length of the half-wave dipole is given as $\qty{20}{\meter}$. Taking the shortening factor into account, the following holds for a half-wave dipole:

$l = \frac{\lambda}{2} \cdot k$.

With the shortening factor $k=\num{0.95}$, the wavelength is

$\lambda = \frac{2l}{k} = \frac{2\cdot\qty{20}{\meter}}{\num{0.95}} \approx \qty{42.11}{\meter}$.

With

$f=\frac{c}{\lambda}$

this gives

$f = \frac{\qty{300}{\mega\meter\per\second}}{\qty{42.11}{\meter}} \approx \qty{7.125}{\mega\hertz}$.

The dipole is therefore resonant at a frequency of about $\qty{7.125}{\mega\hertz}$.
