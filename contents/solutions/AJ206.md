Harmonics lie at integer multiples of the fundamental frequency. To check whether one of the frequencies given is a harmonic of $\qty{144.300}{\mega\hertz}$, the frequency can be divided by the fundamental frequency. If the result is a whole number, it is a harmonic. The ordinal number of the harmonic can be calculated with the following formula:

$N=\frac{f_N}{f_0}$

For $\qty{432.900}{\mega\hertz}$ this gives:

$N=\frac{\qty{432.900}{\mega\hertz}}{\qty{144.300}{\mega\hertz}}=3$

So it is the third harmonic.

For $\qty{1298.700}{\mega\hertz}$ this gives:

$N=\frac{\qty{1298.700}{\mega\hertz}}{\qty{144.300}{\mega\hertz}}=9$

It is the ninth harmonic.

The transmitted signal can therefore cause interference on $\qty{432.900}{\mega\hertz}$ and $\qty{1298.700}{\mega\hertz}$.

For the other frequencies given, the division does not give a whole number. They are therefore not harmonics of the fundamental frequency.
