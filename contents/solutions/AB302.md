[picture:878:a_phasenwinkel_zeit:Phase angle in degrees and radians, or as a fraction of the period]

Figure [ref:a_phasenwinkel_zeit] shows the representation against time and the angle of rotation in degrees and radians. 

* $X_1$ corresponds to $\qty{90}{\degree}$ or $\frac{\pi}{2}$ (see the green phasor B in the figure)
* $X_2$ therefore corresponds to $\qty{180}{\degree}$ or $\pi$ (see the red phasor C in the figure).
* $X_3$ is at $\qty{270}{\degree}$ or $\frac{3\pi}{2}$, i.e. $1.5\cdot\pi$ (see the blue phasor D in the figure). 
* $X_4$ corresponds to $\qty{360}{\degree}$ or $2 \pi$.

The value for $X_3$ can also be found with the rule of three. Look for the whole cycle, or period, at T and count the time intervals belonging to it. In the drawing for the question, there are 4 intervals in one period. A whole period corresponds to $\qty{360}{\degree}$, and one interval then to $\frac{\qty{360}{\degree}}{4} = \qty{90}{\degree}$. The three intervals up to $X_3$ then give $3 \cdot \qty{90}{\degree} = \qty{270}{\degree}$.
