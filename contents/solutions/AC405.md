In the circuit, two silicon diodes are connected in inverse parallel across the output. As a result, they limit the output voltage in both directions.

As long as the voltage stays below about $\qty{0.6}{\volt}$, both diodes block and the signal appears almost unchanged at the output. But if the voltage becomes greater than the forward voltage of a silicon diode, one of the two diodes conducts and limits the voltage to about $\pm\qty{0.6}{\volt}$.

The output signal is therefore cut off at the top and bottom when it is greater than $\qty{0.6}{\volt}$ or less than $-\qty{0.6}{\volt}$.
