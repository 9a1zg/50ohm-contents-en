The Zener diode stabilises the voltage at the left-hand terminal of $R_2$ at

$U_Z=\qty{6.2}{\volt}$

When the wiper of $R_3$ is at end stop 1, the wiper is connected directly to the upper terminal of $R_3$. From this point to chassis there are two branches in parallel:

$R_4=\qty{6.8}{\kilo\ohm}$

and

$R_3+R_6=\qty{220}{\ohm}+\qty{150}{\ohm}=\qty{370}{\ohm}$

The equivalent resistance to chassis is therefore:

$R_\mathrm{u}=R_4\parallel(R_3+R_6)$

$R_\mathrm{u}=\frac{\qty{6800}{\ohm}\cdot\qty{370}{\ohm}}{\qty{6800}{\ohm}+\qty{370}{\ohm}}\approx\qty{351}{\ohm}$

Now $R_2=\qty{270}{\ohm}$ must also be taken into account. $R_2$ and $R_\mathrm{u}$ form a potential divider across the stabilised voltage of $\qty{6.2}{\volt}$:

$U_\mathrm{G}=\qty{6.2}{\volt}\cdot\frac{\qty{351}{\ohm}}{\qty{270}{\ohm}+\qty{351}{\ohm}}\approx\qty{3.5}{\volt}$

Since the sources of the transistors are connected to chassis, the gate voltage is also the gate-source voltage:

$U_\mathrm{GS}\approx\qty{3.5}{\volt}$

The gate-source voltage is therefore about $\qty{3.5}{\volt}$.

Incidentally: 

The resistor $R_5=\qty{51}{\ohm}$ has practically no effect on the DC voltage at the gate, since almost no direct current flows into the gate of the LDMOS transistor. For the RF signal, however, $R_5$ is important: together with the gate capacitance it damps possible radio-frequency oscillations and so improves the stability of the amplifier.

The resistor $R_4=\qty{6.8}{\kilo\ohm}$ ensures that the gate has a defined potential with respect to chassis even if the bias circuit is interrupted. It also discharges the gate capacitance and so prevents the transistor from conducting unintentionally because of a floating gate. Since $R_4$ is in parallel with the lower arm of the potential divider, it must be taken into account when the gate voltage is calculated exactly.
