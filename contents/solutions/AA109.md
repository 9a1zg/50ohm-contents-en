# Given
Transmitter output power: $p_\text{Sender, Ausgang} = \qty{1}{\watt}$  
Gain: $g_\text{Endstufe} = \qty{10}{\dezibel}$  

# Solution
We use the $\unit{\dBm}$ formula from the formula sheet (levels, power and voltage levels):  
$ p = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{P}{\qty{1}{\milli\watt}}\right)\unit{\dBm}$  
and use it to calculate the input level of the power amplifier:
$ p_\text{Endstufe, Eingang} = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{p_\text{Sender, Ausgang}}{\qty{1}{\milli\watt}}\right)\unit{\dBm}= 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{\qty{1}{\watt}}{\qty{10^{-3}}{\watt}}\right)\unit{\dBm} \\ = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(10^{3}\right)\unit{\dBm}= 10 \cdot \qty{3}{\dBm}\\ = \qty{30}{\dBm}$  
The output level then follows by simple addition:
$ p_\text{Ausgang} = p_\text{Eingang} + g_\text{Verstärkung} = \qty{30}{\dBm} + \qty{10}{\dB} = \qty{40}{\dBm}$

# Shortcut
Anyone who has worked a little with logarithms and the table in the examination aids can work out the solution in their head.  
$\qty{1}{\watt}$ corresponds to $\qty{1000}{\milli\watt}$, which corresponds to $\qty{30}{\dBm}$; adding the gain of $\qty{10}{\dezibel}$ gives $\qty{40}{\dBm}$.
