We need the range of the input resistance of the circuit. The $\qty{200}{\ohm}$ resistor on the left is always in series with the rest of the circuit.

After this resistor, the circuit splits into two parallel branches:

* one branch with $\qty{100}{\ohm}$
* one branch with $\qty{200}{\ohm} + R$

First we look at the smallest value. For this we set:

$ R = \qty{0}{\ohm} $

Then $\qty{100}{\ohm}$ is in parallel with $\qty{200}{\ohm}$:

$ R_\mathrm{par} = \frac{\qty{100}{\ohm} \cdot \qty{200}{\ohm}}{\qty{100}{\ohm} + \qty{200}{\ohm}} \approx \qty{67}{\ohm} $

With the resistor in series before it, this gives:

$ R_\mathrm{min} = \qty{200}{\ohm} + \qty{67}{\ohm} = \qty{267}{\ohm} $

Now we look at the largest value. For this we set:

$ R = \qty{1}{\kilo\ohm} $

Then $\qty{100}{\ohm}$ is in parallel with $\qty{200}{\ohm} + \qty{1}{\kilo\ohm}$, i.e. in parallel with $\qty{1200}{\ohm}$:

$ R_\mathrm{par} = \frac{\qty{100}{\ohm} \cdot \qty{1200}{\ohm}}{\qty{100}{\ohm} + \qty{1200}{\ohm}} \approx \qty{92}{\ohm} $

With the resistor in series before it, this gives:

$ R_\mathrm{max} = \qty{200}{\ohm} + \qty{92}{\ohm} = \qty{292}{\ohm} $

So the input resistance lies in the range of about $\qtyrange{267}{292}{\ohm}$.
