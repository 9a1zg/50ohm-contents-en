First the required base current is calculated:

$ I_B = \frac{I_C}{B} $

with

$ I_C = \qty{5}{\milli\ampere} $

and

$ B = 298 $

so:

$ I_B = \frac{\qty{5}{\milli\ampere}}{298} \approx \qty{16.8}{\micro\ampere} $

Not the whole $\qty{12}{\volt}$ is dropped across the resistor $R_1$, but, because of the base-emitter voltage, only:

$ U_{R_1} = \qty{12}{\volt}~-~\qty{0.6}{\volt} = \qty{11.4}{\volt} $

Now, with Ohm's law:

$ R_1 = \frac{U_{R_1}}{I_B} $

$ R_1 = \frac{\qty{11.4}{\volt}}{\qty{16.8}{\micro\ampere}} \approx \qty{679000}{\ohm} $

so approximately:

$ R_1 \approx \qty{680}{\kilo\ohm} $
