In a linear voltage regulator, the excess voltage is converted into heat. 

The power dissipation of a linear voltage regulator is the difference between the input power and the output power:

$ P_{\mathrm{V}} = P_{\mathrm{in}} - P_{\mathrm{out}} $

First we calculate the input power. Since in a linear voltage regulator the input and output currents are approximately equal:

$ P_{\mathrm{in}} = U_{\mathrm{in}} \cdot I = \qty{13.8}{\volt} \cdot \qty{0.9}{\ampere} = \qty{12.42}{\watt} $

Now we calculate the output power:

$ P_{\mathrm{out}} = U_{\mathrm{out}} \cdot I = \qty{9}{\volt} \cdot \qty{0.9}{\ampere} = \qty{8.10}{\watt} $

This gives the power dissipation:

$ P_{\mathrm{V}} = \qty{12.42}{\watt} - \qty{8.10}{\watt} = \qty{4.32}{\watt} $

The power dissipation in the voltage regulator is therefore:

$ P_{\mathrm{V}} = \qty{4.32}{\watt} $
