In this question you must not be misled by the well-known property of a field-effect transistor: with DC the gate draws practically no current and therefore has a very high input impedance. At high frequencies, however, the parasitic capacitances of the transistor play an important part, in particular the capacitances between gate and source and between gate and drain. Their capacitive reactance decreases with increasing frequency, so that an RF current can flow at the gate. With RF power transistors the input impedance can therefore be considerably lower than you would expect from looking at a FET for DC. The input transformer $T_1$ therefore serves to match the $\qty{50}{\ohm}$ to the low input impedance of the transistors. And the output transformer $T_2$ serves to match the low output impedance of the transistors to the $\qty{50}{\ohm}$ of the transmitter that follows.
