The base current $I_B$ follows the input voltage $U_E$. If $U_E$ rises, the base current $I_B$ and the collector current $I_C$ rise too. Because of the larger collector current, the voltage drop across the collector resistor increases, so that the collector-emitter voltage $U_{CE}$ falls. $U_{CE}$ is therefore phase-shifted by $\qty{180}{\degree}$ with respect to $U_E$ and $I_C$.

The coupling capacitor at the output merely removes the DC component. The output voltage $U_A$ therefore has the same phase as the AC component of $U_{CE}$, but is centred on the zero line. So $I_C$ and $U_E$ are in phase, while $U_{CE}$ and $U_A$ are in antiphase to them.
