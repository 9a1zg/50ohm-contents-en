We are looking for the length of *one* of the two wires of the half-wave dipole. Each wire therefore has, to begin with, an electrical length of $\lambda/4$.

The wavelength at $\qty{14.2}{\mega\hertz}$ is

$\lambda = \frac{c}{f} = \frac{\qty{300}{\mega\meter\per\second}}{\qty{14.2}{\mega\hertz}} \approx \qty{21.13}{\meter}$.

A quarter of the wavelength is therefore

$\frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{\qty{21.13}{\meter}}{4} \approx \qty{5.28}{\meter}$.

Now the shortening factor of $\num{0.95}$ must be taken into account:

$l = \qty{5.28}{\meter} \cdot \num{0.95} \approx \qty{5.02}{\meter}$.

The two wires must therefore each be about $\qty{5.02}{\meter}$ long.
