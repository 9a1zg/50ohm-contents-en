The velocity factor of a coaxial cable depends on the dielectric used between the inner and outer conductors.

In a coaxial cable with a solid polyethylene dielectric, the signal travels at only about $\num{66}\,\%$ of the speed of light. The velocity factor is therefore approximately

$k = \num{0.66}$.

A wavelength in the cable is therefore only about $\qty{66}{\percent}$ as long as the corresponding wavelength in free space.
