Since all the resistors have the same value:

$ R_1 = R_2 = R_3 = \qty{10}{\kilo\ohm} $

The current flowing through $R_3$ is:

$ I_{R_3} = \qty{1}{\milli\ampere} $

Since $R_2$ and $R_3$ are connected in parallel and have the same resistance, the current through $R_2$ is also:

$ I_{R_2} = \qty{1}{\milli\ampere} $

The current through $R_1$ is the sum of the two branch currents:

$ I_{R_1} = I_{R_2} + I_{R_3} = \qty{1}{\milli\ampere} + \qty{1}{\milli\ampere} = \qty{2}{\milli\ampere} $

Now we first calculate the equivalent resistance of the parallel connection of $R_2$ and $R_3$:

$ R_{23} = \frac{R_2 \cdot R_3}{R_2 + R_3} = \frac{\qty{10}{\kilo\ohm} \cdot \qty{10}{\kilo\ohm}}
{\qty{10}{\kilo\ohm} + \qty{10}{\kilo\ohm}} = \qty{5}{\kilo\ohm} $

The total resistance of the circuit is therefore:

$ R_\mathrm{ges} = R_1 + R_{23} = \qty{10}{\kilo\ohm} + \qty{5}{\kilo\ohm} = \qty{15}{\kilo\ohm} $

With the total current, the total voltage is:

$ U = I \cdot R = \qty{2}{\milli\ampere} \cdot \qty{15}{\kilo\ohm} = \qty{30}{\volt} $
