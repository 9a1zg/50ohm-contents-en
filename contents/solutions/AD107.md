We are given:

$ U = \qty{15}{\volt} $

and

$ R_1 = R_2 = R_3 = \qty{10}{\kilo\ohm} $

First the equivalent resistance of the parallel connection of $R_2$ and $R_3$ is calculated:

$ R_{23} = \frac{R_2 \cdot R_3}{R_2 + R_3} $

Since both resistors have the same value, this gives:

$ R_{23} = \qty{5}{\kilo\ohm} $

The total resistance of the circuit is therefore:

$ R_\mathrm{ges} = R_1 + R_{23} = \qty{10}{\kilo\ohm} + \qty{5}{\kilo\ohm} = \qty{15}{\kilo\ohm} $

Now the total current can be calculated:

$ I_\mathrm{ges} = \frac{U}{R_\mathrm{ges}} = \frac{\qty{15}{\volt}}{\qty{15}{\kilo\ohm}} = \qty{1}{\milli\ampere} $

This current first flows through $R_1$ and then divides between the two equal resistors $R_2$ and $R_3$.

Since both resistors have the same value, exactly half of the total current flows through each branch:

$ I_3 = \frac{I_\mathrm{ges}}{2} = \frac{\qty{1}{\milli\ampere}}{2} = \qty{0.5}{\milli\ampere} $
