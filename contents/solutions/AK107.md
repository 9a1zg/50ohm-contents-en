## Formulas needed

Formulas to use from the Federal Network Agency's examination aids for calculating the safety distance in the far field:

$d = \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E}$ 

and for the relationship between EIRP and ERP:

$P_\mathrm{EIRP} = P_\mathrm{ERP} \cdot 10^{\frac{g_d + 2{.}15 - a}{10}}$

## Rearranging the formula

$d = \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E}$ 
$d \cdot E = \sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}$
$(d \cdot E)^2 = \qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}$
$\frac{(d \cdot E)^2}{\qty{30}{\ohm}} = P_\mathrm{EIRP}$
$P_\mathrm{EIRP} = \frac{(d \cdot E)^2}{\qty{30}{\ohm}}$

## Data from the question

1. The stated antenna gain relative to the dipole: $g_d = \qty{6}{\dBd}$ 
2. The cable loss is negligible: $a = \qty{0}{\dB}$
3. The safety distance is: $d = \qty{5}{\meter}$
4. The limit for the protection of persons is: $E = \qty{28}{\volt\per\meter}$


## Solution steps

1. Calculating $P_\mathrm{EIRP}$:

$P_\mathrm{EIRP} = \frac{(\qty{5}{\meter} \cdot \qty{28}{\volt\per\meter})^2}{\qty{30}{\ohm}} = \qty{653.33}{\watt}$

2. Calculating $P_\mathrm{ERP}$:

$P_\mathrm{ERP} = \frac{P_\mathrm{EIRP}}{10^{\frac{g_d + 2{.}15 -a}{10}}} = \frac{\qty{653.33}{\watt}}{10^{\frac{6 + 2{.}15 -0}{10}}} = \frac{\qty{653.33}{\watt}}{6.531} \approx \qty{100}{\watt}$

## Interpretation

The maximum output power of the transmitter must not exceed approx. $\qty{100}{\watt}$.
