# Solution by elimination
It helps to recognise that this is the envelope detector shown in the chapter on the demodulator.  
On the left-hand side of the circuit you can see that the IF signal is applied to the input of the circuit there.  
$\rightarrow$ This rules out the answer "the output for the IF signal."

Terminal $\text{X}$ lies after an RC low-pass filter, which is built with an electrolytic capacitor (recognisable by the small "+"). Electrolytic capacitors have a relatively high capacitance. For the relationship between cut-off frequency ($f_\text{g}$) and capacitance ($C$), we look at the formula (filter, RC low-pass) in the formula sheet: 

$f_\text{g} = \frac{1}{2\cdot\pi\cdot R \cdot C}$

A large capacitance ($C$) gives a large denominator and thus a low cut-off frequency ($f_\text{g}$) for the RC low-pass filter.  
$\rightarrow$ This rules out the answers "the output for the AF signal." and "the output for the oscillator signal."

At terminal $\text{X}$ there is a low-frequency voltage that can be used for control.
