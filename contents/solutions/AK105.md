## Approach, part 1
The formula sheet in the Federal Network Agency's examination aids shows, in the section "Levels" (*Pegel*), that an attenuation of $\qty{-6}{\dB}$ corresponds to a factor of 0.25 as a power ratio.

Expressed as a formula:

$P_{\qty{40}{^\circ}} = 0{.}25 \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}$

## Approach, part 2
In the section "Radiated power and gain" (*Strahlungsleistung und Gewinn*) of antennas, the formula sheet in the Federal Network Agency's examination aids gives the following formula:

$E = \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{d}$

Since a safety distance is to be determined, the formula must be rearranged for $d$:

$d = \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E}$

## Substituting

$d_{\qty{40}{^\circ}} = \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_{\qty{40}{^\circ}}}}{E} = \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot 0.25 \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E} = \frac{\sqrt{0.25} \cdot \sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E} = \sqrt{0.25} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E} = 0.5 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E} = 0.5 \cdot d$

## Interpreting the formula

The safety distance at $\qty{40}{^\circ}$ is half the safety distance in the direction of maximum radiation. 
It is therefore reduced from the given value of $\qty{20}{\meter}$ to the required value of $\qty{10}{\meter}$.
