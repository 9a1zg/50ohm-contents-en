Aliasing describes the occurrence of ambiguities when an analogue signal is sampled with an A/D converter. The correct answer therefore concerns the A/D converter. The D/A converter generates a stepped analogue signal and needs a *reconstruction filter* to filter out the wanted frequency range after the D/A conversion.

To decide whether a high-pass or a low-pass filter can be used, we look at the sampling theorem, which can also be found in the examination aids:

$f_\text{abtast} > 2 \cdot f_{\mathrm{max}}$

It states that the highest signal frequency must always be lower than the sampling rate. This cannot be achieved with a high-pass filter, since it passes all frequencies from its cut-off frequency $f_\mathrm{g}$ up to (at least in theory) infinity. A low-pass filter, on the other hand, passes all frequencies from $\qty{0}{\hertz}$ up to its cut-off frequency $f_\mathrm{g}$. A suitably designed low-pass filter can therefore ensure that the sampling theorem is complied with. For this, the cut-off frequency must be well below half the sampling rate.
