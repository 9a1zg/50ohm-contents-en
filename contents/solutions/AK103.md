## Explanation

### Source for calculating the safety distance

The Federal Network Agency's document "Explanation of the assessment procedures under the BEMFV" (*Erläuterung der Bewertungsverfahren nach BEMFV*) of August 2013 provides the answer.

Section 1.2.2 (Carrying out the far-field calculation) contains formula (5) for calculating the safety distance - denoted here by $r$:

$r = \sqrt(\frac{Z_0}{4 \cdot \pi}) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{P \cdot G_i}}{E_g} \cdot C$

With $Z_0 = \qty{120\pi}{\Ohm}$ the first factor simplifies to $\sqrt{\qty{30}{\Ohm}}$. Neglecting the loss factor $C$, we obtain the formula for calculating the safety distance that is given in the question and in the Federal Network Agency's aids:

$d = \frac{\sqrt{\qty{30}{\Ohm} \cdot P_\mathrm{EIRP}}}{E}$

Section 1.2.1 states that the formula given above for calculating the safety distance is valid only in the far field of a source of radiation.

It also distinguishes there between the *reactive near field* and the *radiating near field*.

### Reactive near field

You are in the reactive near field of an antenna if the distance from the antenna is 

$d < \frac{\lambda}{2\pi}$ 

Here the following applies:

"Within the reactive near field there can be strong local increases in the electric and the magnetic field that cannot be determined by the far-field calculation. A far-field calculation is therefore not permissible in this region."

### Radiating near field

You are in the radiating near field of an antenna if 

* you are outside the reactive near field and
* the distance from the antenna is less than $4 \lambda$.

Here the following applies:

"If the far-field formula is applied in the region of the radiating near field, the result for most antenna types is a conservative estimate, that is, the actual field strengths are lower than those calculated. This does not, however, apply to all types of antenna; a magnetic antenna, for example, produces higher field strengths in the near field than those predicted by the far-field formula. Antennas that are geometrically small in relation to the wavelength in particular show this kind of behaviour."

### Near-field calculations

Section 1.3 deals with the possibility of near-field calculation by simulation:

"By using numerical methods, such as those used in what are called near-field calculation programs, it is possible to calculate the electric and magnetic fields exactly, in magnitude and phase, for any point in space around an antenna."

### Interpretation

The answer given above can be justified from the explanations of the reactive and the radiating near field and of near-field calculations.
