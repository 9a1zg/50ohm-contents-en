Unwanted mixing products at the output of a mixer can have both
lower and higher frequencies than the wanted
mixing product. To make sure that only the wanted signal gets into
the transmitter output stage, the mixer must therefore be followed by a
band-pass filter that passes only the wanted signal
and filters out both lower and higher frequencies.

The other answers cannot be correct:

* Coupling out through a high-pass filter would only filter out
  unwanted mixing products below the wanted signal, but not
  unwanted mixing products above the wanted signal.

* Amplifying the signal with a linear
  class A driver stage amplifies all mixing products equally –
  wanted and unwanted alike.

* Coupling out through a *broadband* attenuator attenuates
  all mixing products equally – unwanted and wanted alike.
