When a DC voltage is applied to an inductor, once the transient has died away only the ohmic resistance of its winding acts. The current is therefore limited essentially only by this DC resistance.

With an AC voltage, the inductive reactance $X_L$ is added. It increases the total resistance of the inductor (see the formula sheet):

$X_L = 2\pi\cdot f \cdot L$

As a result, for the same voltage, the current is smaller than with a DC voltage.
