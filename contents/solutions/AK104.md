## Explanation

### Power at the antenna input

The power at the antenna input $P_\mathrm{Ant}$ is the power at the feed point of the antenna. 
It is the power that actually arrives at the antenna after the losses in the feeder.

### Attenuation factor

The calculation uses the attenuation factor $D$, where $0 < D < 1$.  
$D$ is defined as the ratio of the power delivered to the power supplied:
$D = \frac{P_\mathrm{Ant}}{P_\mathrm{Sender}}$
where

* $P_\mathrm{Sender}$: transmitter output power
* $P_\mathrm{Ant}$: power at the antenna input

Rearranging gives the power at the antenna input:

$P_\mathrm{Ant} = D \cdot P_\mathrm{Sender}$

The greater the losses in the feeder, the smaller the attenuation factor and therefore also the power available at the antenna.

### Remember

The power at the antenna input is the transmitter output power multiplied by the attenuation factor of the feeder. 
The attenuation factor describes what proportion of the transmitter power actually arrives at the antenna after the line losses.
