For the question of the narrowest possible bandwidth and the
steepest skirts, the order from good to poor is as
follows:

1. Crystal filter
2. Ceramic filter
3. LC filter
4. RC filter

Crystal filters are the best choice when you need a very narrow filter
(i.e. only a few $\unit{\kilo\hertz}$ or even only a few hundred
$\unit{\hertz}$ wide) with steep skirts (e.g. as an IF filter for CW
or SSB signals).

Ceramic filters work in a similar way to crystal filters, but have
somewhat poorer electrical properties than crystal filters.

LC filters and RC filters are not suitable for applications such as
CW or SSB filters. With them you achieve neither comparably narrow
filter bandwidths nor comparable skirt steepness.
An LC filter has steeper skirts than an RC filter, since the RC filter
contains only one frequency-dependent component (the capacitor),
whereas in the LC filter both components are frequency-dependent.

When rather wide filters are needed, e.g. for a band-pass filter
that is to pass an entire amateur band, crystals or ceramic
resonators are in turn unsuitable, because such wide filters
cannot be made with them. LC filters are used
for this.
