# Solution 1:

A half-wave dipole for this frequency would be about $\qty{315}{\meter}$ long and is therefore hardly feasible for most radio amateurs. In practice, heavily shortened antennas are therefore mostly used, whose efficiency is considerably lower than that of a full-size half-wave dipole. An antenna gain of $\qty{-20}{\dBd}$ is therefore entirely plausible. Since the coaxial cable used is only short, its loss in this frequency range can be neglected.

[Annex 1 to the Amateur Radio Ordinance (Amateurfunkverordnung, AFuV)](https://50ohm.de/a1) already gives a hint to the correct answer to the next question. It specifies a maximum power of $\qty{1}{\watt}$ ERP for the $\qty{630}{\meter}$ band. That is why only $\qty{0.5}{\watt}$ can be the correct answer. 

# Solution 2:

To solve the question, you can use the table of power ratios on the formula sheet. It gives a factor of $\num{0.01}$ for $\qty{-20}{\dB}$.

$\qty{50}{\watt}\cdot 0.01 = \qty{0.5}{\watt}$
