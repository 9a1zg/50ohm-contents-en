First the base current is calculated:

$ I_B = \frac{I_C}{B} = \frac{\qty{2}{\milli\ampere}}{200} = \qty{10}{\micro\ampere} $

Ten times the base current is to flow through $R_2$:

$ I_2 = 10 \cdot I_B = \qty{100}{\micro\ampere} $

The current through $R_1$ is therefore:

$ I_1 = I_2 + I_B = \qty{110}{\micro\ampere} $

$\qty{1}{\volt}$ is dropped across the emitter resistor.  
In addition, the base-emitter junction needs about $\qty{0.6}{\volt}$.

This puts the base voltage at:

$ U_B = \qty{1}{\volt} + \qty{0.6}{\volt} = \qty{1.6}{\volt} $

The voltage across $R_1$ is therefore:

$ U_1 = \qty{10}{\volt} - \qty{1.6}{\volt} = \qty{8.4}{\volt} $

Now, with Ohm's law:

$ R_1 = \frac{U_{R_1}}{I_{R_1}} = \frac{\qty{8.4}{\volt}}{\qty{110}{\micro\ampere}} \approx \qty{76.4}{\kilo\ohm} $
