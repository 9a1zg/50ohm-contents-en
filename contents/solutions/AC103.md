An ideal reactance (capacitive or inductive reactance) has no heat losses.

Reason: in a pure reactance, current and voltage are $\qty{90}{\degree}$ out of phase. As a result, the instantaneous power is alternately positive and negative, and its mean value over one period is zero. So no real power is dissipated, and consequently no heat is produced.
