Let us go through, from left to right, what the circuit does with the watt fed into it.

First there is a parallel connection of $\qty{110}{\ohm}$, $\qty{110}{\ohm}$ and two $\qty{330}{\ohm}$ resistors. We know, of course, that in a $\qty{50}{\ohm}$ system the input resistance must also be $\qty{50}{\ohm}$. If you like, however, you can check this again and calculate the resistor network. 

According to the formula sheet:

$\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{110} + \frac{1}{110} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 330}$

So $R = \frac{660}{13} \approx \qty{50}{\ohm}$

If $\qty{1}{\watt}$ is now fed into this parallel connection, you get: 

$U = \sqrt{P \cdot R} = \sqrt{1 \cdot 50} \approx \sqrt{49} \approx \qty{7}{\volt}$

For the diode only the positive half cycle counts, i.e. the peak voltage:

$\hat{U} = U \cdot \sqrt{2} \approx \qty{10}{\volt}$

The input voltage is halved by the $2 \cdot \qty{330}{\ohm}$ potential divider and then reduced by $U_F$:

$U_A = \frac{\hat{U}}{2} - U_F = \qty{5}{\volt} - \qty{0.23}{\volt} \approx \qty{4.8}{\volt}$
