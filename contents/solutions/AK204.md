## Explanation

Even at relatively low transmit powers of only a few watts, high RF voltages can occur on transmitting antennas and feeders. The hazard depends not only on the power but, to a large extent, on the local voltage and impedance distribution.

The relationship between power, voltage and resistance follows from:

$P= \frac{U^2}{R}$

Where:

* $P$ = power
* $U$ = voltage
* $R$ = impedance (or resistance at the point considered) 

With high or locally strongly varying impedances, especially due to mismatch and standing waves, high RF voltages can occur even at a few watts of transmit power.

In addition, RF voltage is potentially dangerous for the following reasons:

* it can cause skin burns,
* the current through the body produces strong local heating,
* there is often no sensation of a classic electric shock,
* unintended RF flashovers can occur.

Example: on a poorly matched antenna with standing waves, RF voltages in the region of several hundred volts can occur at only 5 to 10 W of transmit power.

There is therefore a relevant hazard from RF voltages on antennas and feeders even at low transmit powers.
